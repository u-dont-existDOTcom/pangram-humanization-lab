# Banned Patterns and Voice Watchlist

The canonical bans in `MASTER-INSTRUCTIONS.md` are absolute for generated article prose except inside quotations, proper names, or literal discussion of the banned term.

## Canonical bans

- No “That's not X. It's Y.”, “not X — it's Y,” or close negative-reveal construction.
- Never use the canonical banned phrases, including “load-bearing,” “that's doing some work,” “tells on itself,” “clean” as praise, vague “work/does its work,” “corollary,” “structure/structurally” as a tic, “the variable that moves it,” “operates at the edges of,” “to be clear,” or repeated “honestly.”
- No formulaic therapy-speak.
- Do not end many consecutive sections with aphorisms.
- Do not repeat a distinctive phrase in one piece.
- If a word or idea appears twice within three paragraphs, cut or consolidate one unless repetition is clearly intentional.

## Common assistant constructions to remove

- “Here's the thing,” “I'll be blunt,” “let's be blunt,” fake-candid “Honestly?”, and other announcements of candor instead of direct candor;
- a list announcement before a short, obvious list;
- padded staccato drama or a rule of three that contains only one real thought;
- successive paragraph-ending aphorisms, antitheses, slogan-like metaphors, or social-media quote-card lines;
- expanded forms such as “would not,” “cannot,” or “it is” throughout an otherwise conversational passage when natural contractions would fit;
- false ranges that imply breadth without naming the actual items;
- interpretive aftercare after a joke, anecdote, image, quotation, or emotional beat;
- conclusion-as-summary or explanation of what the reader learned;
- “pivotal moment,” “testament to,” “marks a turning point,” “rich history,” or similar significance inflation;
- “experts believe,” “research shows,” or “studies say” without a named, linked source;
- synonym cycling instead of the clearest noun;
- “metabolize” or “digest” used metaphorically when plain language is better;
- “serves as,” “boasts,” “features,” or “stands as” when “is” or “has” works;
- “in order to” when “to” works; “due to the fact that” when “because” works;
- **faux-insight setup:** “what nobody tells you,” “the part everyone misses,” or similar authority theater before an ordinary claim;
- **colon reveal:** a noun phrase plus colon used for fake drama rather than a real list, label, quotation, or grammatical relation;
- **participial faux-analysis:** trailing “highlighting,” “underscoring,” “reflecting,” or “showcasing” clauses that restate importance instead of explaining cause or consequence;
- **negative listing:** “Not X. Not Y. Z.” when Z can be stated directly;
- **rhetorical setup:** “What if I told you…,” “Think about it,” “Plot twist,” or self-answered question pairs that manufacture suspense;
- **fake-profound kicker or recap ending:** a mic-drop metaphor, aphorism, or summary after the real point has already landed.

These portable patterns are merged from the no-ai-slop audit. Its blanket bans on emoji headings, em dashes, abstraction, first-person roughness, and broad word classes do not apply here; Joel's article-specific function and voice remain controlling.

## Execution and information-organization failures

Flag or reject:

- **semantic-preflight skip:** rewriting before checking whether the paragraph's actual thought makes sense;
- **manufactured puzzle:** asking a question whose premise already answers it, or creating a contradiction by silently assuming a separate actor/chooser/cause;
- **curiosity-chain break:** the next sentence answers a question an interested reader did not actually have because the prose is following a prebuilt outline;
- **conceptual-space completion:** exhaustive question batteries, taxonomies, or source inventories added because the writer can complete the category rather than because the thought requires them;
- **functional redundancy:** different sentences explain the same significance or relationship even when they share no wording;
- **everything-predicts-everything:** paragraph functions are so orderly that each sentence or paragraph strongly predicts the next one's role;
- **content-writer closure:** useful example → explanatory bridge → generalized takeaway → final summary after curiosity has already ended;
- **neutral questionnaire drift:** live conversational thought flattened into survey/checklist phrasing when a faithful natural expression exists;
- **cold-audit theater:** claiming a post-draft audit passed because the rules were known during generation, without rereading the literal completed output;
- **audit paraphrase loop:** continuing to generate D4/D5/D6 after later audits find no real defect;
- **wrong substrate reconstruction:** discarding AI sentences while retaining only an AI-compressed semantic summary of Joel when richer author material exists;
- **provenance halo:** treating a detector-targeted owner edit as natural owner writing merely because Joel authored the edit or Pangram passed it;
- **detector-green stop error:** ending fidelity/editorial review when Pangram turns Human.

## Corpus-derived tics: watch, do not ban

Joel naturally uses connectors and pivots such as “so,” “but,” “actually,” “keep in mind,” “unfortunately,” “interesting,” “anyway,” “for example,” and rhetorical “right?” These can carry live thought and reader contact. They become synthetic when deliberately sprinkled as signatures or repeated within a short span.

Likewise, parentheses, rhetorical questions, affectionate nicknames, emojis, self-corrections, and jokes are tools—not quotas.

## AI-shaped pattern sweep

Flag when:

- every section has the same length, temperature, and argumentative shape;
- every example confirms the thesis and receives an explanatory moral;
- every transition announces the next section;
- emotion appears mainly as stock bodily cues or weather mirroring mood;
- references sound culturally literate but remain unnamed;
- complex outcomes resolve through acceptance, forgiveness, connection, mindset, healing, or one brave choice;
- the final paragraph adds uplift after the article has ended;
- the draft imitates the cancer article with an unsupported personal opening;
- the draft imitates the tender transcript with unearned nicknames or speech filler;
- the draft imitates dense health guides by stacking claims faster than it separates evidence;
- a polished sentence hides an unsupported first-person belief or experience;
- an off-voice draft has received only a cosmetic restraint pass while keeping the same opening, metaphors, paragraph endings, rhetorical order, and conclusion.

## Mixed-truth, scope, and context failures

Flag:

- a polished reversal that makes real praise sound naive or retracts it merely to introduce harm;
- “the same culture has its shadow” or similar symbolism when the relationship is coexistence rather than causation;
- a Sénégal-, city-, group-, or person-specific observation inflated into “Africa,” society, humanity, or a whole category without evidence;
- an issue repeated in two sections with no change of function;
- a purposeful recurrence deleted merely because keywords repeat;
- an orphaned coined-term definition, section introduction, example, transition, conclusion, link, caption, or media anchor after editing;
- `otherwise`, `also`, `still`, `other`, `same`, `too`, `yet`, or `but` retaining an obsolete logical relation after insertion/reordering;
- a correction applied as surface wording while the rejected judgment survives elsewhere;
- communal warmth treated as proof that conformity, exclusion, or violence cannot coexist with it;
- **conventional-caution closure:** calling an unexplained non-accident, survival, recovery, coincidence, or anomalous event “luck,” “fortunate,” placebo, pathology, or proof of a mechanism merely to sound responsible when several causes remain live;
- **contentious-detail laundering:** deleting an exact political, military, institutional, financial, or historical detail because it is controversial or locally inconvenient while retaining only the smoother moral summary.

## Never “humanize” by degrading the prose

Do not add random errors, slang, contradictions, digressions, disfluency, or factual looseness. Do not make every paragraph eccentric. Preserve Joel's actual material and varied intensity.

Flag or ban:

- exact memories, lyrics, or catchphrases shortened for cadence without permission;
- entity leakage between nearby people, places, communities, or dates;
- vague “same,” “there,” “this,” “that,” “they,” “more,” or “less” after reordering;

- silently changing a substantive claim during a style pass;

## Genre-calibrated density guardrails

Do not ban a visible thesis, framework, repeated qualification, binary contrast, or polished line merely because it resembles an AI-associated feature. Polemics, guides, and research articles may need them. Flag the **concentration** instead:

- nearly every example immediately proves the thesis;
- several paragraphs in succession end on polished aphorisms;
- mirrored “X/Y” construction becomes the article's default rhythm;
- almost every heading is a slogan;
- every complication is absorbed by the framework;
- no real unresolved danger survives;
- the conclusion repeats meaning already earned.

Do not “fix” this by adding errors, random fragments, fake vulnerability, flashbacks, named references, or digressions by checklist. Humanize architecture, not grammar.

## Audience, jargon, and review-interface watchlist — flag, do not automatically ban

Flag for review when the source had clearer language, the reader needs an action, or the article begins serving a different audience:

- role drift toward clinician, lawyer, manager, investor, policymaker, reviewer, academic, or insider without a deliberate reason;
- expertise drift from accessible explanation into disciplinary shorthand;
- goal or venue drift: a guide becomes a literature review, an essay becomes a policy brief, a public article becomes an internal memo, or a personal argument becomes a sales page;
- relationship drift from peer/witness/author into lecturer, therapist, institutional spokesperson, or marketer;
- source jargon, priorities, and assumptions imported from a source written for another audience rather than translated;
- diagnostic-sounding shorthand such as `feels subcortical`, as though a nervous-system level were directly perceived or diagnosed;
- `interoceptive tolerance`, `autonomic downshifting`, `stakeholder alignment`, `capacity building`, `operationalization`, or comparable prestige terminology replacing a clearer description of what people notice or do;
- `qi`, prana, energetic, biomedical, neurobiological, legal, financial, academic, or technical terms introduced before the local passage establishes which vocabulary the intended reader needs;
- vague `integration`, `containment`, `regulation`, `implementation`, `alignment`, `accountability`, `resilience`, `governance`, `inclusion`, or `support` without actor, action, timing, threshold, stop criterion, or outcome where practical guidance requires them;
- third-person professional language replacing a direct `you`, `we`, named group, or concrete actor from the source;
- stating that a posture, technique, process, policy, or practice has no correct form when the intended point is that several valid adaptations exist within constraints;
- editor overlay text such as `Double click to interact with video` copied into article prose, diffs, or review cells;
- a newer rewrite being retained merely because it sounds more technical or professional, when the original is clearer or more exact.
- an author-developed sequence, taxonomy, checklist, scorecard, or combined framework presented as though the complete synthesis were externally validated merely because some components are;
- a warning or accusation widened by dropping conditions, population, jurisdiction, consent, timing, intensity, or uncertainty modifiers;
- “there is no correct version” or equivalent anything-goes language when the real point is that several adaptations remain valid within constraints.

These are context-sensitive watch items. Preserve a technical term when it is accurate, necessary, defined at the point of need, and suited to the audience contract.

- **magic-word generalization:** promoting one minimal pair into `word A = human` or `word B = AI` without cross-case recurrence and holdout survival;
- **sentence-only minimal-pair distortion:** dropping the full tested boundary when a one-token change affected a longer passage;
- **pseudo-precise micro-verdict:** assigning percentages to a very short excerpt when the evidence supports only a qualitative judgment.

These are cluster and context findings. A precise word, caveat, first-person phrase, or symmetrical sentence may be fully justified. Diagnose the operation and local architecture, not the token in isolation.
