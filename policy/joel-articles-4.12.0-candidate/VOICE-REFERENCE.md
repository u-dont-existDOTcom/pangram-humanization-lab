# Current Voice Reference

Read this after the canonical master instructions. It converts Joel's recent writing and spoken material into operational guidance. It is a decision model, not a phrase bank.

## Source hierarchy

- **Long-form mixed-register calibration:** no single current community artifact is authoritative. Use owner-final passages and labeled before/after comparisons only when their provenance is clear. Do not treat the deleted community PDF, the earlier community draft, or any assistant-produced revision as proof of successful humanization.
- **Primary research calibration:** the cancer article and “Do Your Own Research.” These best represent evidence discussion, self-correction, and reader address.
- **Primary tender calibration:** the inner-child video transcript. Use its emotional stance and conversational intimacy, not its raw speech disfluencies.
- **Secondary practical calibration:** the DMSO and autism articles. Use them for dense guide rhythm, prioritization, caveats, and practical organization. Do not treat their density or claim volume as the default for narrative essays.
- **Legacy calibration:** older Blogspot writing reveals durable instincts but not the current standard. Use legacy material only when a current instruction or approved sample confirms the trait still survives.
- **Anti-sample/source repository:** the earlier community draft. It preserves facts and ideas but demonstrates generic compression, quote-card density, invented architecture, and over-determined closure. Use only in the paired case study.

## Stable voice qualities

### Start where the actual thought starts

Lead with the point, problem, scene, or personal stake. Joel often explains why he cares before building the argument, but he does not need a ceremonial hook. A direct question is useful when it opens the real inquiry rather than manufacturing suspense.

### Let motive sections remain motive sections

A heading such as **“Why I’m publishing this”** may begin with reluctance, cost, duty, or the odd fact that Joel would rather be doing almost anything else. These details are not a ceremonial personal hook when they explain why publication became necessary. Do not replace motive with a novelty statement, a miniature literature review, or a thesis preview.

In source-rich intellectual history, show the actual field before the bibliography: what broad classes of writers could and could not say, who reported experience without understanding it well, which person came closest, and why that person mattered to Joel. Give attention according to dependence, consequence, and changed judgment. One personally consequential teacher may need a full paragraph while several corroborating thinkers need one sentence each.

Visible self-implication may involve mistaken deference: Joel initially assumes his own failure to understand is the problem, then later concludes that the admired source was also confused. Preserve that revision when it carries the authority and humility of the section. Do not simulate it with a generic “What I hadn’t realized…” transition.

### Let the reasoning remain visible

The prose often shows the mind revising itself: what Joel used to think, what new evidence changed, what remains unclear, what he found ironic, and what still needs testing. Preserve this visible process. Do not clean it into a falsely final expert voice.

### Speak to one intelligent reader

Use “you” naturally. Ask real rhetorical questions. Anticipate the obvious objection or confusion in ordinary language. Do not lecture an abstract audience or complete every inference for the reader.

Preserve the article's audience contract: who that reader is, what they know, why they are reading, what they may decide or do, the stakes, and the publication relationship. Do not let a citation, reviewer, technical source, or polished rewrite turn the reader into a clinician, academic, investor, policymaker, insider, or abstract “general audience” unless the article deliberately changes audience.

### Put a human or mechanism where a category would be

Joel's strongest passages name the person, relationship, object, sequence, rule, institution, bodily process, or exact research problem. Replace abstractions with who did what, what changed, and why it matters.

### Use humor as pressure release and diagnosis

Humor often appears beside grief, danger, spiritual intensity, or argument. It releases pressure, exposes absurdity, or admits the writer's own oddness. The most characteristic humor comes from concrete absurdity, self-implication, and one unexpectedly specific detail—the moment where describing what is actually happening makes the situation ridiculous. It is not a quota, a stand-up routine, or decoration. A casual aside may be more authentic than a polished punchline. Do not make successive paragraphs culminate in aphorisms, antitheses, slogan-like metaphors, or quote-card lines.

### Keep intensity uneven

Some paragraphs are blunt; some are plain connective prose; some are technical; some are tender; some contain one strange joke. Do not make every sentence perform the voice. Uniform distinctiveness feels synthetic.

### Preserve mixed judgments

Joel often means two things at once: a culture can be unusually warm and dangerous for a minority; a family obligation can be loving and coercive; a spiritual tradition can preserve real wisdom and still concentrate authority. Keep both truths active. Do not turn admiration into a setup for a clever reversal or make one moral fact swallow the other.

### Leave unexplained causality open

Joel's writing can hold a firm practical judgment beside an unresolved explanation. “Do not drive during this” may be warranted even when nobody knows why prior episodes caused no accident. Do not supply a conventional closure such as luck, coincidence, placebo, pathology, or providence unless the evidence or Joel supports it. Name the serious alternatives in ordinary language and let the unknown remain alive.

### Preserve contentious specificity

An exact political, military, institutional, or historical fact may be part of the human route rather than a detachable research aside. Keep the detail when it explains why a person felt morally trapped, why a later conversion mattered, or why Joel responded as he did. Handle verification separately from voice. Do not genericize the history merely to make the paragraph smoother or safer.

### Keep scope local until evidence broadens it

Use the specific country, place, community, person, or institution Joel actually discussed. A local observation can suggest a wider question, but it does not become a continental or universal claim by rhetorical momentum.

### Recurrence can be functional

Joel may revisit one issue because it does a different job later: first as lived contradiction, later as a design criterion. Preserve that recurrence when the function changes; avoid repeating the same conclusion twice.

## Cadence and mechanics

- Use contractions by default in conversational prose: “wouldn’t,” “can’t,” “it’s,” “don’t,” “I’m,” and similar forms. Expanded forms should sound intentional—used for emphasis, quotation fidelity, solemnity, or a genuinely formal passage—not like generic assistant prose. Controlled full-boundary tests show that expanding one natural contraction can materially change detector classification, but this remains a contextual rhythm effect rather than `contractions = human`.
- Preserve conversationally sufficient modifiers. Do not replace ordinary `just`, `really`, `quite`, `somehow`, `even`, `still`, or `probably` with `exactly`, `precisely`, `specifically`, `particularly`, or another more analytical synonym merely to sound polished. Check whether the substitution changes certainty, closes natural ambiguity, or turns an experienced reaction into a completed explanation.
- Mostly medium-length conversational sentences, interrupted by short questions, blunt conclusions, parentheses, and occasional fragments.
- “So,” “but,” “keep in mind,” “unfortunately,” “interesting,” “anyway,” and “for example” are natural connectors, not mandatory signatures. Remove them when they become filler or repeat too quickly.
- Parentheses can carry jokes, qualifications, self-correction, or important side information. Do not stack so many that the main sentence disappears.
- Rhetorical questions should express real curiosity, incredulity, or reader guidance. Avoid a metronomic question-answer pattern.
- Em-dashes and ellipses may remain when they reflect the source draft. Do not normalize them away automatically.
- Paragraphs usually carry one or two beats. Dense guide lists may be longer when the sequence genuinely belongs together. In analytical prose, do not flatten several related evidence types into one neat comma-series merely because they can all be named as nouns. A three-item list is fine when it is the real list; when two items are one kind of evidence and the third is another, let the syntax show that relation.
- Emojis are proactive editorial tools, not a quota: use well-chosen universally rendering symbols for warmth, emphasis, navigation, scanning, headings, practical steps, callouts, and CTAs; allow them in tender passages when meaningful; keep evidence-heavy passages uncluttered.

## Register 1: heavy / irreverent

Use for polemic, culture critique, institutional criticism, and sharp argument.

- State the judgment plainly and name the mechanism.
- Use absurdity, irony, or a pointed question to expose contradiction.
- Keep Joel in the frame when relevant: what he saw, believed, missed, changed, or found funny.
- Distinguish a strong moral judgment from an empirical claim. Bluntness does not grant factual certainty.
- Avoid generic rage, grand conspiracy atmosphere, caps-lock escalation, insult stacking, or an accusation that outruns the evidence.

The effect should be: a specific human arguing because the stakes matter, not an anonymous polemic generator.

## Register 2: direct / tender

Use for grief, family, childhood, emotional pain, spiritual care, and confession.

Joel's tenderness is **playful, nurturing, concrete, philosophically serious, and protective**. It is not solemn by default.

- Address one hurt person directly rather than producing “therapeutic content.”
- Begin with contact, reassurance, a simple question, or a concrete action before theory.
- Use ordinary emotional words. Directly naming love, grief, fear, hurt, loneliness, or relief is often stronger than bodily symbolism.
- Affectionate language can work when it is already part of the relationship or article world. Do not sprinkle “lovey bunny” into unrelated writing as a brand tic.
- Repetition may reassure, clarify, or hold attention. Retain purposeful repetition but remove transcript loops and accidental filler.
- Tenderness can include correction, boundaries, or a difficult truth. Do not confuse kindness with soft conclusions.
- Humor may interrupt seriousness at exactly the moment a conventional writer would become reverent. Preserve it when it feels earned.
- Do not become purple, saccharine, inspirational, clinical, or formulaically validating.

### Spoken tenderness into written prose

When adapting speech, preserve:

- the emotional stance;
- reader contact;
- concrete soothing gestures;
- plain definitions;
- protective firmness;
- the natural move from warmth to philosophy and back;
- comic self-awareness.

Usually remove:

- repeated “you know,” “right,” “so,” and false starts;
- abandoned clauses;
- accidental loops;
- side tangents caused by extemporaneous speech;
- explanations that only compensate for the absence of editing.

The written result should feel spoken-to, not transcribed.

## Register 3: research-conversational

Use for science, health, history, technology, and heterodox research.

- State why the research matters, especially when a personal stake was supplied.
- Show changes of mind and uncertainty. “I thought X; the stronger evidence changed that” is more Joel-like than a seamless authoritative verdict.
- Explain mechanisms in ordinary language, then state the evidence level separately.
- Name the study, author, institution, population, comparison, and outcome when they matter.
- Compare mainstream consensus, heterodox practice, mechanistic plausibility, anecdotes, and speculation without merging them.
- Rank options or claims when that helps the reader, but explain the ranking criteria.
- Admit access limits, missing full texts, absent data, conflicts, and unresolved questions.
- Critique both mainstream and alternative camps where warranted. Do not manufacture symmetry.
- Treat “luck” and other conventional explanations as hypotheses rather than default neutral facts. A safety recommendation can be stronger than the causal conclusion.
- Use personal experience as a lead, illustration, or hypothesis—not as a substitute for external evidence.

## Register 4: practical guide

Use for protocols, checklists, tools, and harm-reduction sections.

- Make the next action or decision obvious.
- Put warnings close to the action they govern.
- Explain why a precaution matters in normal language.
- Distinguish “what I did,” “what others report,” “what a mechanism suggests,” and “what human evidence supports.”
- Use headings and lists for navigation, but do not turn every paragraph into a card or micro-section.
- Preserve useful specificity while avoiding false precision.
- Do not let the accumulating list of caveats bury the central path.

## What not to imitate from the samples

- Factual claims, citations, links, allegations, treatment claims, doses, or recommendations: all require fresh verification.
- Raw transcript disfluency.
- Old claim-stacking, speculative causal chains, caps, exclamation clusters, or universal certainty.
- Every recurring verbal connector as a mandatory tic.
- Every unusual nickname, joke, metaphysical claim, or controversy in unrelated subject matter.
- Dense catalog form when the article needs narrative movement.

## Voice-preservation priorities during editing

Preserve, in order:

1. supplied facts and personal truth;
2. distinctive lines, jokes, and odd but meaningful details;
3. the emotional temperature and reason for writing;
4. the sequence by which Joel actually reached the point;
5. useful qualifications and self-corrections;
6. punctuation and emoji choices that carry tone.

Smooth only what obscures meaning, repeats without purpose, weakens evidence, or turns a human passage into generic prose. If the passage is already AI-shaped at the architectural level, do not preserve its clever metaphors or sentence sequence merely because they are polished; return to the underlying material and reconstruct it.

### Author-intent interviews are source pools

When Joel answers questions to recover a softened or misframed passage, preserve his actual thought and strongest language without treating the answers as a transcript, sentence bank, or mandatory inventory. Archive the exact answers, then build the section's pressure, intellectual/lived route, causal chain, contradiction, governing movement, paragraph jobs, and stopping point independently of question order.

Draft first from that architecture in fresh syntax. Return to the raw answers afterward to restore owner-final/locked wording, exact memories and quotations, coined terms, semantically precise phrases, and distinctive lines that perform a necessary job. Do not stitch clauses from several answers merely to sound like Joel. Exact archival preservation and exact publication reuse are different decisions.

Select, consolidate, move, and synthesize into coherent finished prose. Competent editing is required. Generic normalization, balanced model explanation, tidy taxonomy, copied-answer collage, and invented morals remain prohibited.


## Sustained lightheartedness

In a long personal essay, lightness may be a baseline current rather than a few isolated jokes. It can live in connective prose, headings, captions, observations, and practical sections while child safety, acute medical risk, trauma, legality, and abuse remain comparatively straight. Do not make the article read like a report with three conspicuous joke insertions.

## Catchphrase and idiolect use

Read `VOICE-LEXICON.md`. Exact phrases are context-bound and sometimes intonation-dependent. Preserve them when supplied, but never use them as a shortcut to voice. The current article's own objects, rules, and absurdities are more important than a familiar phrase.

## Growth beyond the corpus

The corpus records who Joel is now; it does not require repeating every weakness. Suggest clearer sequencing, stronger evidence, more varied pacing, better scene construction, or a more useful image plan when needed. Calibrate improvements on one section and preserve the real worldview, humor mechanism, and human material.
