# Transformation Case Study — Coherence-First Author-Intent Recovery, Detector Repair, and Layered Attribution

This is the canonical v4.12.0-candidate workflow for recovering Joel's thought after AI drafting or polishing. Read it with `HUMANIZATION-AND-COHERENCE.md`. It replaces any reading of “exact or near-exact author language” that produces transcript collage, preserves an AI-imposed argument, sacrifices coherence, or turns archival fidelity into copy/paste dependence. It supplements the edit contract, claim-preservation rules, correction-propagation rules, locked-block protections, and layered-attribution workflow.

## 0. 4.12 execution finding: the bottleneck is often failure to inspect the completed output

The r33/r34 work established an important process distinction. Many previously documented humanization principles were already correct; the recurrent failure was that the model generated prose while “knowing” them but did not run a genuine second inference over the completed passage before delivery.

A successful post-draft audit can remove defects that are obvious only once the whole object exists at once: a final summary after the thought already landed; two semantically different sentences doing the same interpretive job; an exhaustive question battery; generic bridges; overly balanced pairings; or fidelity/actor errors introduced during drafting. The major improvement may occur in the first cold audit; later audits can still matter editorially even after a detector is fully Human.

This does **not** mean cold auditing can rescue every globally model-shaped architecture. r35–r37 supplied the counterlesson: ritualistically editing a detector-Human passage can make it worse, and repeatedly polishing a globally AI-shaped argument merely produces nicer versions of the same conceptual diagram. Before auditing, decide whether there is a good passage underneath that can be exposed by deletion/local repair. If not, reject the architecture and reconstruct from the richest actual Joel/source material.

The resulting execution order is:

**semantic sanity → architecture decision → curious-reader chain → draft → cold audit 1 → cold audit 2 → optional cold audit 3 → fidelity/coherence gate → detector last**.

The curious-reader chain is literal: after every sentence ask what an interested reader genuinely wants to know next. If the draft repeatedly answers a different question because an outline already dictates the next function, the prose remains model-organized even when it contains Joel-specific facts.

## 1. Governing rule: author-language fidelity is not transcript fidelity

Treat an author-intent interview as a **source pool**, not a required content inventory or publication sequence.

Preserve:

- Joel's central judgment;
- substantive claims and their certainty;
- factual, causal, doctrinal, and phenomenological distinctions;
- unusual or uncomfortable conclusions that AI softened;
- high-value examples that carry the argument;
- exact phrases whose wording is distinctive and functionally important;
- meaningful uncertainty, asymmetry, qualification, repetition, and register shifts;
- actor → action → object assignments;
- links, source attributions, media anchors, and other required context.

Do not automatically preserve:

- the order in which Joel answered questions;
- every example or speculative branch;
- brainstorming alternatives;
- repeated explanations;
- drafting-process notes;
- research-discovery provenance;
- material included only because a prompt asked about it;
- details that are distracting, excessively explicit, tonally wrong, or unnecessary to the section's function;
- catchy lines that do not fit the finished passage;
- an inherited AI heading, causal sequence, transition, conclusion, or section architecture.

“Do not translate Joel back into model prose” means:

- do not soften or normalize his position;
- do not replace his reasoning with balanced generic explanation;
- do not invent symmetrical frameworks, concessions, metaphors, morals, or mini-conclusions;
- do not convert an unusual judgment into a respectable abstraction.

It does **not** mean:

- preserve transcript syntax;
- avoid transitions;
- avoid synthesis;
- avoid paragraph development;
- retain every answer;
- follow interview-question order;
- refuse competent selection, consolidation, movement, or clarity work.

The target is **Joel's actual thought expressed as coherent finished prose**.

## Raw-source preservation is not publication reuse

Preserve exact interview answers, rough notes, rejected branches, and corrections in the source record. Do not infer that every preserved sentence must appear verbatim in the article. Owner-final or explicitly locked prose remains authoritative; raw elicitation material must be selected and composed.

Any substantial exact span reused from raw interview or brainstorm material needs a private reason: owner lock, exact memory/quotation/formula, identity-bearing language, semantic precision, or temporary placeholder. A clause mosaic assembled from several answers is not author fidelity.

## Architecture reconstruction before prose

Before writing sentences, complete the coherence architecture card from `HUMANIZATION-AND-COHERENCE.md`. Map the real pressure, reader stake, controlling claim, intellectual/lived route, actor relations, causality/chronology, evidence roles, contradiction, governing movement, paragraph jobs, stopping point, and exact-language reasons.

The outline must be independent of interview-question order, source order, and inherited model headings. A genuine multi-function passage may retain several purposes inside one governing movement.

## Fresh-syntax composition and paragraph-chain audit

Draft first from the architecture card and claim/evidence maps, then compare against the raw source to restore justified exact language and audit omissions. Each paragraph must inherit something, change the reader's understanding or pressure, and create the next move. If paragraphs correspond one-to-one with answers or sources and can be freely shuffled, the section is not yet composed.

## 2. Classify the source pool before reconstruction

| Class | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Locked claim or correction | Must survive in meaning, actor assignment, causality, and certainty |
| Distinctive author language | Preserve when it performs a useful job in the finished passage |
| High-value example | Retain where it carries or complicates the argument |
| Optional illustration | Select only when it improves comprehension, force, pacing, or humor |
| Purposeful repetition | Retain when the second statement deepens, complicates, or changes the function of the first |
| Brainstorming alternative | Not publication material by default |
| Process provenance | Keep outside Joel's byline unless the article explicitly discusses its creation |
| External evidence | Integrate only for a defined evidentiary or explanatory function |
| Redundant explanation | Consolidate or omit |
| Risky or overexplicit detail | Generalize or omit when the substantive claim survives and permission allows |
| Model-written bridge | Keep only when natural, necessary, faithful, and coherent |
| Detector-passing but owner-corrected text | Joel's correction controls. Pure P1 corrections retain prior detector status; substantive visible or boundary changes require retesting before a new lock. |
| Extracted text missing links or media context | Consult the authoritative HTML or original context before judging the wording |

Omitting optional interview material does not violate author-language fidelity. Omitting or changing a substantive claim, certainty marker, causal relationship, exact memory, or locked phrase still requires authority under the edit contract.

## Coherence gate before detector testing

A candidate does not advance to detector testing until it works without the interview, works with its actual neighbors, preserves the intellectual route and exact uncertainty, has a paragraph chain, uses exact raw language only for recorded reasons, dispositions unused value, and stops where the thought arrives. A detector score cannot repair a failed composition gate.

## 3. Preserve unused value without creating transcript collage

**Governing rule: author-intent selection is not author-intent disposal.**

Every substantive answer elicited during author-intent recovery receives one explicit disposition:

1. **Use now** — it belongs in the current passage and performs a necessary function.
2. **Bank for a named later destination** — it is valuable, but belongs in another section, appendix, heading, caption, conclusion, glossary, or derivative.
3. **Preserve as project-level context or evidence** — it controls judgment, certainty, terminology, chronology, or future corrections without necessarily belonging in published prose.
4. **Omit with reason** — it is redundant, brainstorming-only, distracting, excessively detailed, unsupported, tonally wrong, or outside the article's scope.

Create an **Author-Intent Carry-Forward Bank** immediately when useful material does not belong in the current passage. Preserve Joel's exact raw answer in the bank even when a later publication version may be selected, consolidated, generalized, or rewritten. “Optional or omitted material + reason” is not enough when the material may have a later destination. Bank it before deciding on permanent omission.

Do not force valuable information into the current section merely because the interview surfaced it. “Not used now” does not mean rejected. Do not preserve every detail in prose merely for traceability, and do not postpone the disposition until the material has been forgotten. A substantive claim, correction, exact memory, unusual judgment, causal distinction, actor assignment, or certainty marker may not be permanently omitted without the authority required by the edit contract.

### Progressive-disclosure routing

The main passage receives the minimum needed to understand its claim. A later section may receive the practical consequence or personal evidence. A technical appendix may receive doctrinal detail, textual disputes, case studies, and alternatives. Project context may retain speculative or background material that the article does not need. Repeat a banked point in more than one destination only when its rhetorical function changes.

Before drafting each later section, consult entries assigned to that section or function. Re-evaluate whether each item still belongs; combine related items into coherent prose; record the exact destination after integration. Do not draft in Bank-ID order, reproduce interview-question order, or empty the bank merely to clear it. The bank protects information; it does not dictate architecture.

### Final carry-forward audit

Before final article delivery, resolve every banked item as integrated, explicitly deferred, project-context-only, superseded, or omitted with reason and the required approval. Ask:

- Did any substantive correction fail to reach the article or a controlling ledger?
- Did a useful example, distinction, caveat, anecdote, source, metaphor, or exact phrase disappear only because it did not fit the first reconstruction?
- Did a later section independently recreate a weaker version of something already banked?
- Did another passage make an item redundant or create a conflict?
- Does any item still have `needs-destination` or `needs-Joel-decision` status?
- Would integration improve the article, or merely bloat it?
- Was every substantive permanent omission actually authorized?

Joel's own deletion in an authoritative rewrite is `owner-deleted/resolved`. Do not bank it, restore it at final assembly, or treat it as an assistant omission unless Joel explicitly assigns it elsewhere.

## 4. Run a semantic-reset gate before writing

An AI-shaped passage may contain the wrong thought, not merely the wrong wording. Author-intent questions can also inherit the AI passage's false assumptions.

Before reconstructing, verify:

1. What is Joel actually trying to say?
2. Does he still endorse the passage's central claim?
3. Did the AI soften a real disagreement into a vocabulary difference or polite caveat?
4. Did it strengthen a tentative interpretation into a confident claim?
5. Did it reverse or reassign the actor, action, object, chronology, or cause?
6. Did it attribute a result to one source when Joel meant a combination?
7. Does the heading make a causal claim Joel rejects?
8. Did PDF or plaintext extraction remove a link that supplied the sentence's referent?
9. Is an AI-added clarification substantively wrong?
10. Should this passage still exist in anything like its current form?

Do not force Joel's answers back into the inherited architecture merely because the questions were based on it.

When Joel says, in effect, **“This was not even saying what I meant,”** discard the inherited progression and reconstruct from the corrected thought. Do not preserve omitted material, old transitions, or prior actor assignments unless Joel restores them.

A direct Joel rewrite supersedes every earlier model candidate, even when the earlier candidate received a stronger detector result.

### Semantic corrections exposed during detector repair

- **Wrong causal heading:** “The Map That Got Me Past Oneness” overstated what happened. “The Map Showed Me What Existed Past Oneness” preserved the real sequence: the map showed the destination; Joel still had to do the practice.
- **Softened disagreement:** “Our vocabulary is not identical” concealed Joel's actual judgment that the other account was usually wrong about ordinary functioning without sensory unification.
- **Wrong actor:** A model version made Joel search backward for the first universe. Joel clarified that he was no longer there as the agent in that experience.
- **Wrong doctrinal clarification:** “Pain remains pain” was an AI-added defense that did not express Joel's intended distinction about direct nibbāna.

Detector repair that changes a claim, actor, certainty, cause, heading, attribution, or omission becomes a substantive correction. Record it and run the normal dependency and correction-propagation audits.

## 5. Coherence and publishability precede detector repair

After recovering the thought, first build the best article passage supported by the material.

Before drafting, identify:

- the section's central claim;
- what causes what;
- what merely permits, enables, or accompanies the result;
- the strongest complication or countercondition;
- the intended reader and register;
- the required progression;
- the strongest examples;
- the evidence levels;
- where qualifications belong;
- the honest stopping point.

Build the section outline independently of interview-question order.

Assign every retained item a rhetorical function:

- establish the claim;
- define a needed term;
- report lived experience;
- show a mechanism;
- provide evidence;
- complicate the claim;
- answer a foreseeable misunderstanding;
- supply humor;
- narrow scope;
- transition to the next live question.

Reject any draft that reads like:

- reordered interview answers;
- a transcript collage;
- a sequence of isolated declarations;
- a container for every supplied thought;
- a polished model essay with Joel's nouns substituted into it;
- a series of examples followed by model-written morals;
- a tidy taxonomy that absorbs every complication;
- a passage whose bridges exist only because the interview was conducted question by question.

Read the passage without looking at the interview. Then read it with the paragraph before and after it.

Ask:

- Does the opening establish the real question?
- Does each paragraph perform one intelligible job?
- Does each heading mark a genuine shift in function or register?
- Does every example support or complicate the local point?
- Are first-person experience, doctrine, interpretation, and external evidence distinguishable?
- Is any sentence present only for traceability to an interview answer?
- Is drafting-process information leaking into Joel's byline?
- Are caveats interrupting the positive thesis before the reader understands it?
- Is a point repeated without a changed function?
- Does the ending stop where the thought arrives rather than manufacture a conclusion?
- Would this passage remain coherent, useful, and publishable if no detector existed?

Only after this gate passes should GPTZero, Pangram, or another detector be used for localization.

## 6. Exercise real editorial judgment after the interview

### Opening thesis paragraphs need pressure before abstraction

Opening thesis paragraphs are especially vulnerable when they stack abstract claims before the reader encounters what those claims cost, change, or explain in lived terms. Keep the thesis. State it more bluntly, let the pressure or consequence appear earlier, and avoid completing every explanatory sentence in the opening. The target is fewer sealed abstractions, not a fake anecdote or an arbitrary concrete noun. Exact abstract or metaphysical language remains when it carries Joel's real thought.

### Select specificity rather than accumulating it

More concrete detail is not automatically more human or more useful. Preserve details that carry the argument, reveal the actual experience, create necessary humor, or distinguish Joel's position. Omit details that merely increase volume.

A tidy list of concrete actions can remain model-shaped:

> “talk, cross a road, recognize a friend, and answer a message”

This packaged the concept as a generic functional definition.

Joel's lived explanation passed:

> “By ‘unification,’ I mean I'm having one single experience. It's not ten fingers hitting keys while I have no idea what the fingers are doing.”

Concreteness works when it expresses the actual thought, not when it is installed as a humanizing technique.

### Preserve useful complication

Do not force a perfect rule when Joel supplied a counterexample. In the unification passage, fingers could type without conscious supervision while a witness was still present. That complication clarified the distinction instead of weakening it.

### Use headings when functions or registers change

A long mixed-register section may need headings so personal experience, science, humor, argument, and scope do not depend on constant model-written bridge sentences. Headings should organize real functions, not manufacture a tidy taxonomy.

### Group caveats where possible

State the positive model first. Add a caveat where a reader would otherwise misunderstand the immediate claim, or collect several scope limits in a short later section. Do not interrupt every sentence with defensive qualification.

### Integrate research by rhetorical function

A source may:

- distinguish fantasy from real-life desire;
- support a specific physiological pathway;
- document a historical claim;
- provide terminology;
- offer a materially different interpretation.

Do not credit one source with Joel's complete synthesis. Do not mention which AI tool found the source unless the article explicitly concerns the research process.

**Source provenance is not drafting provenance.**

### Use natural semantic paragraphs

Do not fragment every thought into one-sentence paragraphs merely to avoid AI cadence. A paragraph may naturally contain claim, explanation, example, and qualification. Break at actual semantic seams, not according to a maximum-sentence ritual.

### Complete metaphors that the argument depends on

When a metaphor or relational model remains, make its parts and limits coherent. Do not scatter one half through unrelated paragraphs or leave the reader to infer who does what.

### Catchy lines remain optional

A line may have originated with Joel and still be wrong for the finished section. Preserve it only when its wording or function is genuinely locked. Claim preservation does not require slogan preservation.

## 7. Do not turn detector-associated features into mechanical rules

The following are neither automatic problems nor automatic solutions:

- abstraction;
- technical vocabulary;
- metaphysical language;
- first person;
- rhetorical questions;
- concrete examples;
- jokes;
- short sentences;
- long sentences;
- paragraph length;
- unusual phrasing;
- contractions;
- emojis;
- imperfect or informal syntax.

An abstract passage can test fully human when it expresses Joel's actual layered thought:

> “‘Already perfect without you’ means the problems are all your problems. When you're gone, where are the problems? But it's deeper than no problems. It's literally the experience of infinite perfections experiencing themselves as flows.”

A concrete passage can still test as AI when it follows a neat definition → examples → conclusion template.

Do not repair abstract prose by adding concrete nouns, biography, first person, or humor unless the thought itself calls for them. Do not normalize exact strange phenomenology merely because conventional prose would state it differently.

## 8. Interpret detector results at three levels

Treat these as separate diagnostic signals:

1. **Document or section classification** — useful for detecting an AI-shaped overall architecture.
2. **Sentence shading** — useful for finding likely local pressure points.
3. **Split and boundary tests** — useful for determining whether the problem is a sentence, its order, or the transition between blocks.

These signals can conflict.

- A section classified as fully AI may contain neutral or green sentences because the architecture remains model-generated.
- A sentence that is green in one context may become orange after its neighbors change.
- A low document-level AI probability does not mean every remaining orange island should be ignored.
- A high document-level AI probability does not authorize rewriting coherent owner-approved prose.
- Sentence color does not establish authorship, quality, accuracy, or permission.

Before interpreting the detector, remove or identify:

- duplicated sentences;
- accidental repeated phrases;
- PDF extraction errors;
- OCR corruption;
- missing punctuation;
- stripped hyperlinks;
- editor overlay text;
- scan artifacts.

Mechanical corruption and detector findings are different problems.

## 9. Lock exact passages and their boundaries

A detector lock applies to the exact tested and owner-approved unit, including where relevant:

- wording;
- punctuation;
- capitalization;
- emoji;
- paragraph breaks;
- heading;
- hyperlink and linked phrase;
- immediate lead-in;
- immediate exit sentence;
- order relative to neighboring blocks.

Do not treat a single green sentence as independently locked when the section-level architecture is still failing.

Once a block is coherent, owner-approved, and passing:

- do not rewrite it because a neighboring block fails;
- do not include it in a regenerated full passage unless required for a boundary test;
- do not polish its punctuation or replace its emoji casually;
- do not move it without retesting the new boundary.

When Joel edits a passing block, his version becomes authoritative. Pure P1 spelling, punctuation, capitalization, spacing, literal-agreement, and broken-link repairs retain the prior detector status when visible semantic wording and tested boundaries do not change. Retest substantive wording, paragraphing, order, emoji, link-anchor, or boundary changes before calling the new unit detector-locked.

When adjacent material changes, test the combined boundary before concluding that the unchanged sentence itself has become defective.

An exact detector lock does not freeze an obvious typo, duplicated punctuation, spacing defect, literal-agreement error, or broken link. Correct a pure P1 defect surgically, record it, and retain the prior detector status. Retest only if visible semantic wording, structure, order, link-anchor text, emoji, or the tested boundary changes.

## 10. Preserve and compare every candidate

For each test, retain:

- original passage;
- each model candidate;
- each Joel correction;
- overall detector result;
- sentence shading or highlighted spans;
- exact test boundaries.

If a new candidate performs worse or becomes less coherent:

1. stop advancing from it;
2. return to the strongest prior **coherent and faithful** candidate;
3. preserve already-passing material;
4. localize the remaining failure again;
5. ask for Joel's wording when the model has exhausted its reconstruction allowance.

Do not treat revision order as progress. Newer wording is not presumed better.

A score increase does not outweigh a semantic regression. A score decrease does not automatically disprove an editorial improvement. Coherence, fidelity, and Joel's judgment remain controlling.

## 11. Reconstruction ceiling

Use this sequence:

1. One surgical reconstruction from the existing passage. This does not count as a new architecture when it only repairs local wording.
2. If the passage remains generic, softened, incoherent, or detector-heavy, ask targeted author-intent questions.
3. Build one coherent post-interview architecture from the source pool.
4. When that architecture fails globally, one genuinely different architecture test is permitted. It must change the progression, not merely the wording.
5. If both architecture-level candidates fail globally, revert to the strongest coherent and faithful baseline and ask Joel to rewrite the section from his thought. No third model architecture.
6. After Joel rewrites, make only necessary cleanup and preserve his architecture unless he requests more editing.

Never continue producing increasingly conversational paraphrases of the same model-shaped passage.

## 12. Maintain a cumulative detector-learning ledger

After **every tested candidate**, record:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Passage ID and boundaries | Exact first and last sentence tested |
| Candidate/version | Exact wording or stable version label |
| Detector result | Overall label/probability and sentence shading |
| Change from prior candidate | Exact substantive and rhetorical differences |
| Coherence result | pass / fail + reason |
| Fidelity result | pass / fail + any claim, actor, certainty, or causal issue |
| Locked material | Exact block and boundary context |
| Suspected cause | Architecture, bridge, abstraction, list compression, boundary, wrong thought, extraction error, etc. |
| Confidence | low / medium / high |
| Scope of lesson | local hypothesis / repeated pattern / promoted rule |
| Semantic correction discovered | claim, cause, actor, heading, attribution, link referent, or none |
| Next action | lock / boundary test / author questions / revert / author rewrite / stop |

During live iterative work, report one compact lesson after each result and apply the accumulated lessons before drafting the next candidate.

Do not promote a one-off detector result into a universal writing rule. Mark it as local until it recurs across materially different passages.

Examples:

- Changing “Maybe it's just a hilarious misunderstanding!” to “Maybe we're just misunderstanding it. That would be hilarious.” supports a local hypothesis that the original compressed the thought and reaction into a polished epigram. It does not establish that all humor must use two sentences.
- One technical metaphysical passage passing at 100% human shows that abstraction itself is not the problem. It does not prove that all abstract passages are safe.

## 13. Humor must perform an article function

Humor may:

- release pressure;
- expose absurdity;
- limit a claim;
- admit Joel's uncertainty or limitation;
- deepen the concrete example;
- carry reader contact.

Strong examples from the audit:

> “Maybe we're just misunderstanding it. That would be hilarious.”

The reaction follows the thought instead of packaging both into one polished slogan.

> “Nobody had hired me to peer-review the Big Bounce Theory.”

The joke limits the cosmological claim and ends the passage; it does not add a moral.

> “It was not subtle.”

The plain anticlimax worked better than the stylized inversion “Subtle they were not.”

Do not derive a formula from these examples. Preserve the underlying function.

## 14. Purposeful repetition may deepen rather than duplicate

Do not remove repetition merely because nearby sentences use the same opening or concept.

Joel's passage used:

> “I became one with the universe…”

followed by:

> “I became the entire universe…”

The second sentence intensified and specified the first. It was not an accidental thesis echo. Judge recurrence by function and development, not keyword repetition.

## 15. Owner correction outranks detector success

A passage may score 100% human and still need correction for:

- accuracy;
- causal force;
- emphasis;
- paragraph grouping;
- scope;
- qualification;
- register;
- who is being criticized;
- what the passage is actually for.

Do not defend a model candidate because it passed.

When Joel changes a passing section:

1. accept the correction as authoritative;
2. identify the judgment the correction protects;
3. apply that judgment to analogous passages;
4. preserve the new paragraph grouping and ordering;
5. retain prior detector status for a pure P1 correction; retest substantive visible or boundary changes when detector locking matters.

Detector success is never approval.

## 16. Required coherence audit before every detector test

Before submitting a reconstructed section for testing, confirm:

- the real claim is present;
- the causal model is correct;
- actor/action/object relations are correct;
- chronology is intelligible;
- headings do not overstate causality;
- links retain their referents;
- each paragraph has a live function;
- examples belong where placed;
- complications remain;
- process provenance is absent;
- research and lived experience are separated;
- the passage does not follow interview-question order by inertia;
- no model-written moral follows a point that has already landed;
- no passing neighboring block was casually rewritten;
- the passage flows into and out of its surrounding article context;
- the passage would be worth publishing without a detector.

A detector test performed before this audit is premature.

## 17. Updated end-to-end workflow

1. Preserve the original and inspect its full context, including links and media.
2. Remove or identify duplicated text, extraction errors, and scan corruption.
3. Classify the source: Joel-shaped with local polish / mixed throughout / generated from the premise upward.
4. Try one surgical reconstruction.
5. If it fails, ask targeted author-intent questions.
6. Classify Joel's answers as locked claims, distinctive language, examples, optional material, process notes, evidence, and redundancies.
7. Assign every substantive answer a disposition: use now, bank for a named destination, project context, or omit with reason. Bank valuable unused material immediately.
8. Run the semantic-reset gate; verify that the inherited claim, actor, cause, heading, and sequence are actually intended.
9. State the section's central claim and causal model.
10. Assign each retained item a rhetorical function.
11. Build a coherent outline independent of question order.
12. Check the opening thesis paragraph for abstraction stacking; preserve the claim while bringing pressure or consequence earlier when needed.
13. Choose headings where function or register changes.
14. Draft finished prose using Joel's thought and strongest language.
15. Remove process provenance, redundant examples, unnecessary caveats, and material included only for traceability.
16. Run coherence, audience, evidence-level, actor, causality, link, repetition, scope, and surrounding-boundary audits.
17. Test the coherent passage with the detector.
18. Record the result in the cumulative learning ledger.
19. Lock exact coherent passing blocks and their boundaries.
20. Use split or boundary tests to isolate remaining failures.
21. If a post-interview candidate fails or regresses, revert to the strongest prior coherent candidate and ask Joel for the remaining wording.
22. Before drafting each later section, consult destination-relevant carry-forward entries and record integrations or changed dispositions.
23. Before final delivery, run the carry-forward audit and resolve every item or record an explicit Joel-approved deferral.
24. Stop when no local editorial defect remains. Never trade intelligibility, accuracy, or Joel's judgment for a global score.

## 18. Additional banned failure patterns

Flag or ban:

- **transcript collage:** arranging interview answers with light connectors while failing to create finished prose;
- **question-order drafting:** allowing the sequence of interview questions to determine article architecture;
- **question-frame contamination:** treating assumptions embedded in the AI passage or the model's questions as though Joel confirmed them;
- **wrong-thought preservation:** rewriting the prose while retaining a claim Joel did not mean;
- **semantic detector repair:** changing actor, causality, certainty, doctrine, attribution, or scope without reporting a substantive correction;
- **concreteness superstition:** replacing abstraction with lists, names, or physical details merely because concrete language is assumed to test as human;
- **abstraction panic:** deleting exact technical or metaphysical language that carries Joel's real thought;
- **sentence-color literalism:** treating green as automatically locked or orange as automatically defective without checking section architecture and boundaries;
- **boundary amnesia:** changing neighboring sentences and then blaming an unchanged locked sentence when its detector color changes;
- **candidate-regression drift:** continuing from a worse candidate rather than reverting to the strongest coherent baseline;
- **score-over-owner:** retaining model wording because it passed after Joel corrected its meaning, order, or emphasis;
- **process-provenance leakage:** mentioning which AI found a source or helped draft the passage when that information has no article function;
- **link-context loss:** rewriting a phrase as vague or incomplete because PDF/plaintext extraction hid its hyperlink or native context;
- **mechanical-corruption inference:** treating OCR errors, duplicate sentences, stripped punctuation, or scan artifacts as evidence about voice;
- **isolated-line locking inside generated architecture:** protecting one neutral sentence while leaving the full premise-upward model sequence intact;
- **lessonless iteration:** producing successive detector candidates without recording what changed, what happened, and whether the inferred lesson is local or generalizable;
- **newer-is-better bias:** assuming the latest paraphrase is the correct baseline after its score, coherence, or fidelity regresses;
- **detector-approved inaccuracy:** treating a human score as permission to retain a factual, causal, doctrinal, or experiential error.

## 19. Section provenance, approval, and later recheck

Track each accepted section as one of:

- `owner-authored untouched`;
- `owner-edited final`;
- `assistant-produced owner-accepted`;
- `owner-final available only in scan/PDF`;
- `superseded assistant candidate`.

Acceptance, detector success, and authorship are separate states. Moving on after a passing test does not convert assistant prose into owner-written prose. Put every `assistant-produced owner-accepted` section into a mandatory later recheck queue for full-context voice, fact/link, banned-pattern, source-boundary, and neighboring-transition review before publication. A later Joel line edit may promote it to `owner-edited final`. Pure P1 corrections retain prior detector status; substantive visible or boundary changes do not.

## 20. Source boundaries, section advancement, and author questions

Before claiming that nothing was omitted or that a section is complete, inspect the complete authoritative source span plus the heading immediately before and after it. A `NEXT_SECTION_SOURCE` or handoff excerpt identifies the current target; it does not prove the article boundary. Say “No claims in the supplied block were removed” when that is all that was checked.

In section-by-section work, the assistant locates the next section from the active article, project sources, file library, or handoff packet before asking Joel to send it. When the source already states an idea, quote or accurately summarize the existing claim and ask whether Joel confirms, rejects, or corrects it. Do not ask him to reconstruct information already available. When two placements are possible, recommend one and state the tradeoff.

Maintain the omission audit throughout the live workflow rather than rebuilding it at the end. After each pass, record newly omitted, generalized, moved, certainty-changed, media-separated, restored, owner-deleted, or superseded material; then advance to the next source analysis or exact question set.

## 21. Open options, mixed judgments, and pressure-bearing examples

Lists, categories, and explicit questions may be fully human and necessary. Distinguish an author's provisional options—“one honest option,” “a few I could think of,” “start the ball rolling”—from a closed model taxonomy in which every category is complete, symmetrical, and uniformly explained. Preserve asymmetry, personally weighted phrases, contradictions, and room for the reader to invent another answer.

Exploratory reader contact can replace formal setup when it arises from Joel's actual relation to the question. Do not mechanize the surface devices that happened to pass: affectionate address, ellipses, jokes, questions, lists, and informal grammar remain context-bound. A hypothetical example belongs when it exposes the future pressure or causal consequence of an option; a generic example added for concreteness does not.

### Romance case lessons retained locally

- In **“Two Pillars Dont Hold The Roof Up,”** the friend-network mechanism precedes the dyad thesis. Community also provides epistemic containment: people who know both partners can resist private demonization. Preserve the chronology from deepening bond, to shallower outside ties, to survival dependence, possessiveness, and demonization after refusal. Separate and shared confidants create different risks; meetup advice arrives after the failure is understood. Humor such as the hat-model line carries the burden argument rather than decorating it.
- In **“Why marriage vows are dishonest,”** target the promise of future feeling rather than monogamy itself. The B. experiment, mate-finding switch, 99% result, and jealousy continuing afterward test the conceptual claim. Jealousy may be insecurity while also indicating that the relationship is insecure. Use direct vulnerable language and preserve low-stakes attraction disclosure as preparation for higher-stakes honesty. Stop on the love/form distinction without adding a universal prescription.
- In **“What are you actually choosing?”**, practical partnership may feel wrong to Joel while remaining not inherently wrong. Community matters through the actual causal burden: survival concentrated in the dyad makes romantic trouble threaten the rest of life and can make opening the heart harder. Article-native references may bridge ideal and action when they belong to Joel's thought-world. The practical ending may become a playful exercise without explaining the moral afterward.

## 22. Meta-writing and research-summary architecture

Appendix openers, prefaces, methodology notes, and source-orientation prose are a distinct detector-risk class. The generic progression—why detail was moved, permission to skip, invitation to continue, summary of contents—often remains globally model-shaped even when its sentences are individually harmless. Recover the real reader, burden, suspicion, and epistemic purpose: who will inspect this, why the detail is separated, and what a skeptical reader should concede.

Research-summary architecture can also remain globally AI-shaped while every claim is accurate. Recover why Joel included the source, what question it changed, what supports him, what resists him, and what remains unresolved. Do not invent that Joel read, noticed, agreed with, disagreed with, or was surprised by a source. Match the prose to the actual access level. When a newly supplied full source materially changes an abstract-based treatment, retire the old candidate and remap the source's functions before drafting.

## 22A. Canonical case — “Why I’m publishing this” and the reluctant-synthesis route

This case controls future reconstruction of motive sections. Three assistant candidates were globally classified as AI despite locally neutral or green source-summary sentences. All three retained the same hidden route:

> prior belief that nobody had assembled the model → balanced “how close/how far” qualification → one contribution per thinker → statement of the missing synthesis → complete doctrinal conclusion

The wording changed, but the section function did not. The heading asked **why Joel was publishing**. The assistant kept answering **what was novel**.

Joel then rewrote the complete section from scratch. The supplied GPTZero Advanced Scan screenshot dated 2026-08-03 showed **AI 0% / Mixed 0% / Human 100%** for the full passage. Detector evidence remains secondary; the passage is a canonical case because it is also more coherent, more faithful, and more informative. Factual and doctrinal verification remain separate.

### Owner-authored rewrite

> **Why I’m publishing this**
>
> Maybe it isn't obvious to you, but I'm not one of those people who like to write for the sake of writing. I might as well clean the kitchen for the sake of cleaning even if it's not dirty. If someone else could write this, I would prefer to sip my lemonade and meditate, maybe even pay them a few bucks to do it. But over the past 15 years of reading Buddhist books and all kinds of spiritual writings from many sources, I realized that nobody had put the pieces together coherently, although some had gotten surprisingly close.
>
> The Buddhist monks wrote books that seemed like summaries of the suttas, with none of their own realizations and meditative experiences. Then I realized why: the Vinaya (Buddhist rules of monastics) puts real limits on what they can say publicly about attainments.
>
> Lay teachers were more willing to describe their experiences. The problem there was almost the opposite. They would say what had happened, but their experiences (or the way they understood them) often did not seem to match the suttas very well, and tended toward pre-Buddhist insights as a result.
>
> John Tan, the inspiration between AwakeningToReality.com, was the closest I found. But it was really difficult to understand precisely what he was saying, even after I myself attained doubtless realization of nibbana thanks to reading his experiences and thanks to Soh Wei Yu, his student and popularizer. For quite a while, I assumed the confusion I had about his writings was just from failing to understand how profound he was. Eventually, I realized I was not the one who was confused about his profundity. He was confused himself, and he even recognized that to some extent in hindsight, but the recognition was often piecemeal, through his sprawling journals, posts, and conversations with Soh.
>
> So, I was still left with pieces of the puzzle from everyone else even after I was sure I saw the full picture, which is a strange place to be at for someone not seeking the spotlight. I can't claim that I realized anything beyond what the Buddha taught, but his teaching is sprawling, since he never wrote a book. The Buddha originated the idea of separating the sense doors, and traces contact into craving, becoming, and birth. Culadasa, aka John Yates, a controversial modern lay meditation teacher, described binding processes that construct a self and a world. U. G. Krishnamurti, another lay teacher in recent past, said the senses can operate without a coordinator. Ajahn Buddhadāsa, a 20th century Thai Buddhist monk, said the “I” is born at sense contact. Dōgen, the 5th century founder of Soto Zen in Japan, lets “the myriad things experience themselves.”
>
> But none of them, as far as I have found, gives the synthesis plainly as I am proposing here:
>
> Nibbāna, the ultimate reality Buddha pointed at, is what remains when the whole functioning body-world-witness construction is gone and only the underlying flows remain in their blissfully perfect, parallel multiplicity. Every form of recognition, meaning, agency, and function belongs to the bound construction of saṃsāra, the cycle of birth and death.

Preserve the owner text as the comparison artifact; do not treat its facts as verified merely because its prose is owner-authored or detector-green.

### What changed at the architecture level

1. **The heading regained control.** The section now explains why a reluctant writer feels obligated to publish. The kitchen, lemonade, meditation, and willingness to pay someone are causal motive, not decorative human texture.
2. **The source list became a source landscape.** Monastic books and lay teachers have opposite failure modes before individual names appear.
3. **Attention became unequal for a reason.** John Tan receives the most space because he shaped Joel's realization, self-doubt, and later correction. He is not one citation among five.
4. **Visible reasoning became actual revision.** Joel first attributed the confusion to his own failure to understand someone more profound. Later he reassigned some of that confusion to John Tan. Dependence, admiration, self-implication, criticism, and partial recognition remain active together.
5. **Humanization required expansion.** The successful owner version is much longer because the missing material was the section: publication reluctance, fifteen years of reading, institutional constraint, the opposite lay-teacher failure, source dependence, mistaken deference, and the strange position of being left with the synthesis.
6. **Repeated motifs changed function.** “Nobody put it together,” “pieces of the puzzle,” and “none gives the synthesis plainly” serve motive, lived result, and exact originality claim. They are not keyword duplicates.
7. **Abstraction was not the problem.** The final doctrinal paragraph remains highly abstract and categorical. It works because the preceding route earns it. Do not repair an unearned abstract conclusion by automatically simplifying the conclusion.

### Questions the assistant should have asked

Do not ask which source first revealed the model when Joel already had the model. Ask:

- Why would Joel rather not be writing this?
- What did monastic writing consistently omit, and why?
- What did lay teachers provide, and what was wrong or incomplete about it?
- Who came closest, and why did that person matter personally?
- Did Joel initially think his own failure to understand was the problem?
- Why is being left to state the synthesis an uncomfortable or strange position?

### Promoted rule

For motive headings, recover the cost and obligation behind publication before summarizing novelty. For source-rich intellectual history, organize by real failure modes, chronology, dependence, mistaken deference, revised judgment, and unequal importance. A balanced bibliography plus a polished synthesis is not a substitute for that route. Expansion is justified when the omitted motive and source history are the actual substance.

## 22B. Canonical local-interference cases — caveat grammar and list topology

These cases correct a recurring overdiagnosis: a coherent paragraph can fail because of a highly local statistical pattern, and one failed paraphrase does not prove that the whole architecture must be discarded.

### Near-death comparison: same paragraph, four caveats

The original paragraph used a formal evidence sequence and tested AI. A conversational paraphrase preserved the same reasoning route and also tested AI, which initially led to the mistaken conclusion that the architecture itself was irredeemable. Joel then isolated the caveat inside one otherwise fixed under-100-word paragraph:

| Caveat | GPTZero AI |
|---|---:|
| `They aren't proof of my experience` | 5% |
| `They are not proof of my experience` | 20% |
| `They don't prove what happened to me` | 27% |
| `They do not prove what happened to me` | 49% |

The detector explicitly warned that the sample was under 100 words. The case does not establish a universal contraction rule. It shows an interaction between natural contraction and the degree to which the caveat explicitly completes the evidentiary inference. The most detector-human form was also the most idiomatic and least procedurally defensive, but it must still pass fidelity because `not proof of my experience` is not semantically identical in every context to `does not prove what happened`.

### Suffering paragraph: same evidence, different list topology

A full passage was high-confidence human on GPTZero and Pangram with:

> automatic action, skilled performance, play, and split-brain patients

Removing `play` created the compact academic triad:

> automatic action, skilled performance, and split-brain patients

Pangram moved to about 80% AI, and GPTZero also worsened. Yet three-item lists containing `play` remained human; `automatic action, dreaming, and split-brain patients` was reported as 100% human; and the same three scientific phrases became human when their relation was expressed instead of flattened into one coordinate list:

> automatic action and skilled performance, along with research on split-brain patients

The promoted lesson is **syntax × apparent semantic-class interaction**, not `three items = AI`. A flat `A, B, and C` series can make several related technical phrases look like a retrospectively completed taxonomy. Relation-aware syntax can preserve every item while showing that two are adjacent performance literatures and the third is a different clinical evidence stream.

### Required repair order

When the passage is already coherent and faithful:

1. preserve the strongest coherent baseline;
2. test one local variable at a time across the complete boundary;
3. inspect contraction/negation, nominal versus verbal caveat, coordinate-list topology, exact phrase order, and neighboring boundaries;
4. preserve the exact item set when testing syntax;
5. reject any detector gain that adds an irrelevant item, removes evidence, changes certainty, or worsens prose;
6. reconsider global architecture only after plausible local interference has been ruled out.

A public list of “AI phrases” may be useful as a watchlist, but never as a blacklist. Models can overproduce ordinary human phrases until detectors treat them as statistical evidence. The skill must therefore record exact configurations, counterexamples, and retirement conditions rather than teaching writers that a normal phrase has become intrinsically artificial.

## 23. Evidence-stream independence and component attribution

Consilience requires independent evidence streams. Record whether experience preceded reading, whether scripture or theory independently predicted the observation, whether science was selected after the thesis, and where the streams diverge. One source may both support a shared mechanism and supply the strongest countermodel.

Attribute at component level: primary text, lived event, established theory, source-specific interpretation, Joel's complete synthesis, analogy, and unresolved question. Do not reduce an established theoretical component to Joel's private idea, and do not credit that theory with the whole autobiographical, multisensory, body-world, or appropriation model.

Practice instruction, phenomenological rationale, analogy, mechanism hypothesis, scientific evidence, and proof of the larger claim are separate jobs. A useful exercise does not become evidence because a scientific explanation can be attached to it. State the strongest scientific limitation at the exact disputed level rather than adding a generic hard-problem caveat.

## 24. Source-language and mechanism-step discipline

When modern English terms do not map cleanly onto source-language categories, temporarily use neutral labels outside the article. Search primary-language formulas and grammar, compare translations and parallels, and distinguish:

1. inability to express the distinction;
2. ability to express it syntactically without dedicated nouns;
3. differently overlapping source categories;
4. translator flattening.

Negative textual claims remain provisional until neighboring formula families, synonyms, and parallels have been audited. Keep common access, cross-sensory binding, unified experience, reflective knowing, self-ascription, appropriation, and a permanent witness distinct. Preserve the exact modifier identifying what ceases or is released. Scope a direct episode or meditation state so it is not mistaken for permanent ordinary functioning. A respondent who supports one local point while rejecting Joel's ontology is support for that point, not corroboration of the whole model.

## 26. Layered attribution remains in force

The coherence-first amendment does not weaken the source/experience/interpretation/mechanism distinctions developed during the Nibbāna work.

Keep seven layers distinct:

1. primary text;
2. later commentary or scholarship;
3. Joel's lived experience;
4. Joel's developed interpretation;
5. proposed cognitive or sensory mechanism;
6. scientific analogy or evidence;
7. unresolved alternatives.

A paragraph may combine them, but one level cannot silently validate another. A corrected attribution must propagate through title, subtitle, opening, thesis, section introductions, repeated explanations, headings, table of contents, glossary, summaries, technical sections, analogies, diagrams, captions, jokes, conclusions, links, and short forms. Search literal phrases and nonliteral equivalents.

When one source was over-credited, preserve the strongest surviving model: reassign support precisely, reduce certainty only where required, keep the lived event separate from what texts establish, and preserve live alternatives when the mechanism remains unresolved.

For main-article plus technical-appendix designs:

- the main article carries the experience, minimum decisive distinctions, and enough evidence to understand the claim;
- the appendix carries the full source dispute, philology, alternatives, source hierarchy, and detailed mechanism;
- a point may recur only when its function changes;
- cross-reference rather than re-proving;
- after moving detail, audit definitions, transitions, conclusions, links, headings, and examples for orphaning.

## 27. Preserve conversationally sufficient modifiers

A model may create an AI-shaped result through a one-word “improvement.” Do not replace ordinary `just`, `really`, `quite`, `somehow`, `even`, `still`, or `probably` with `exactly`, `precisely`, `specifically`, `particularly`, or a similar analytical synonym merely for polish. Test whether the new word strengthens certainty, makes the relation more explicit, closes natural ambiguity, or turns reported experience into retrospective explanation.

The current controlled case changed only `just` to `exactly` inside an approximately 300-word passage and reportedly moved GPTZero from strong-confidence human to strong-confidence AI. The lesson is the operation—unnecessary precision upgrade—not a universal token rule. Preserve the full boundary and test recurrence elsewhere.

## 28. Cold short-excerpt audits precede provenance and quality

When classification itself is the task, do not let Joel-specific content, correctness, caution, or owner agreement determine prose shape. Neutralize the content and inspect whether the remaining claim–qualification architecture is portable. Report prose shape, thought provenance, and fidelity/editorial quality separately. A faithful passage may still sound completely AI-written.

## 29. Complete-passage delivery

For passage work, return the complete revised passage ready to paste. Fragment-only splice instructions are reserved for a genuinely isolated paragraph change or an extremely long document. Split tests are temporary diagnostics; the next article candidate remains complete.
