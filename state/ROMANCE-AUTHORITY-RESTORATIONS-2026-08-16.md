## Can Casual Sex or a Situationship Actually Be Honest?

“I don’t know whether I want something serious with you. I’m not pretending I do. I’d still like to have sex.”

Okay. But sex can make a child. If this girl gets pregnant, she’s gonna call you. Then what? You block her? Agree to child support? Try to convince her to abort when she thinks abortion is killing her child? This is what penetrative sex can do. No contraception is perfect.

The STI conversation is easy by comparison: say what you know, or admit you don’t know. Feelings are harder. Getting attached is one of the things sex does. Feelings developing is basically what you’d expect unless one or both people are really numb or robotic.

Outside a loving poly community or tribe, I think honest casual sex is almost impossible. You have to care what happens to the person afterward. Otherwise you’re using and abusing them for pleasure. Maybe they’re using you too. Congratulations, now it’s mutual.

The full conversation is so big that almost nobody would have casual sex if they took it seriously. Which is why almost nobody has the full conversation.

A lot of “I only want fun” really means: this is the only version of the relationship that gets me laid. Sometimes the hope that it becomes love is conscious. Sometimes it’s hiding from the person who has it. The deliberate lie and the self-deception can be the same lie at different depths.

And then there’s the situationship, which is hard to define because that’s basically the point.

Usually it’s a happenstance relationship based on sex. There’s less communication, and what communication exists is more superficial. It feels like it’s filling a relationship void, but only accidentally and occasionally.

I had something sort of like that with H., although ours was much deeper than what people normally mean by situationship. We were BFFs and spent nearly all our time together. She was falling in love with me, and I wasn’t falling in love with her. She felt more like a sister to me, partly because I’m not attracted to white women. Ours wasn’t superficial. It was just painfully uneven.

The more ordinary situationship is obviously some kind of relationship even while one or both people refuse to name it. You’re regularly having sex, going out together, spending time alone together, and doing things you wouldn’t do with a regular friend. It may fill the relationship void just enough to keep you from looking for anything deeper.

It can go either way. Often the man wants to keep it undefined while the woman wants commitment, partly because she’s the one risking pregnancy. Whoever keeps it undefined gets the fun without the responsibility. What they don’t escape is the existential void created by another superficial fix. Doomscrolling entertains you too.

And “Why are you making this heavy?” can be a real question. They may actually mean: don’t you want the fun, and are you about to give it up? My answer would be: I’m trying to understand whether this is really fulfilling me or if it’s drainingly superficial.

But here's the funny thing many people don't understand: labels don't actually change anything. 

True commitment grows out of relational depth, not a label. If we spend hours telling each other our deepest longings, fears, and problems, I’ll naturally be ready to help when you need me. I don’t need to promise that on paper. If someone says “I love you, I feel you, I’m here for you,” yet they hardly ever talk to you about what matters, that's a red flag.

## Decide What You Really Want

The person getting more of what they want may think the arrangement is fulfilling. Usually they’re just less aware that it isn’t. In college I had a friend with four girlfriends who boasted about it to girls while trying to get a fifth. I asked him, “But are you really happy?” He said, “No, I’m miserable, dude. I feel empty inside.”

I can’t decide what is okay for anybody else. I can say that most people who defend casual sex are not being very honest with themselves about pregnancy, attachment, or what they might owe the person afterward.

If you want something closer to “casual love-making” without quite so many ways to damage each other, you probably need to find a free-love community where the people aren’t disposable, the bonds can be acknowledged, and any children have a village.

### Exact owner-final block

Then there are practical partnerships.

I travel the world as a (white) guy from a first-world country. Many women have wanted to marry me partly because they figured I could improve their status and comfort. That always makes me feel sad and lonely to think about, but yeah, I know it's to be expected.

It’s reasonable for a woman to want support, since after all she has to provide for her potential children, but it muddies the waters for me. I'm not stupid, I understand that she might really love me while still wanting money, stability, children, a visa, a home, etc. Men aren't a different species, although sometimes I think I might be. A man can really love a woman for who she is while also seeing her as a source of sex, children, social legitimacy, or help running the house. Affection and practical motives often coexist, even if it isn't the romantic ideal.

And hey, sometimes being objectified is kinda sexy, too. Men like to be objectified to a certain degree as providers and defenders, just as women often like being seen as sex dolls by the men they want. Being wanted that way feels good, but feeling held is important as well.

I don’t want to be just a provider-and-defender machine, because that's vulnerable to hypergamy. I wanna know that if I fall ill or lose my job, I can count on my wife still. Guys want a ride-or-die chick. A woman also wants to know that when she gets old, sick, or no longer looks like the fantasy that first attracted her man, he will still happily stay.

But beyond all that practical stuff, sex is extremely important for building and renewing the connection. I have also said that if two people aren't having sex, it becomes hard to explain how they're different from regular friends. There is a real contradiction here. I don't think it needs to be solved into a rule.

I think sex is especially important while the bond is forming. It may be what turns a friendship into a romance and keeps feeding the romantic connection. But if the connection gets deep enough, sex appeal stops being the basis of the relationship. You don’t stop loving the person because she got old, he lost his job, one of you got sick, or sex became impossible.

Sometimes they may even want you to find sexual satisfaction elsewhere if they can’t give it, because they love you and want you to be happy. Maybe they don’t. Nobody is owed that as proof of love.

No easy answers here in modern life.
