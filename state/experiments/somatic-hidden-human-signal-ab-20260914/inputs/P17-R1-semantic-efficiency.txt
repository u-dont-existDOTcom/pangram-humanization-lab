Those therapies meaningfully reduce hyperarousal for many people, and sometimes dissociation. But Peter Levine et al. identify two problems. On the one hand, exposure desensitization can itself be re-traumatizing, a risk the positive evidence may understate because dropout reasons and adverse events were often poorly or inconsistently reported. On the other hand, even effective trauma-memory approaches can create new cognitive-behavioral patterns that suppress conditioned fear responses without changing the body’s underlying understanding. Symptoms can improve without resolving the root problem.
