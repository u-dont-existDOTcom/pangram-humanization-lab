The inner child is you in a sense, but it's not you. People identify with their inner child. And then if you are the inner child, that means you're going to act like a child. You're not going to be able to think logically. You're not going to be able to plan.

In my inner-child work I call the adult roles the Nurturer and Protector. Sometimes I can't find either one yet. Then I use what I call borrowed adulthood: I take the position of a steady adult for a while, even if I don't actually feel steady.

You can be the perfect parent of your inner child because you do have time to think. When your inner child is hurting, you can think about this. You can say, OK, let me take some time, give me like a few minutes. And then you can say, hey, how are you doing? Love you, bunny.

And if it feels too painful, you can say just feather touch. Just feather touch and then come back to it when you're ready to come back to it. You just keep going like this until you get deeper and deeper. It's not a forceful thing.
