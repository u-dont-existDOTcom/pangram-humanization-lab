Building Enough Safety to Stay Present

Somatic Experiencing

If getting near the material knocks me out of myself, I don't start with the story. I can feel what is happening in my body, look around the room, catch an impulse to run or push away or freeze, and slow the whole thing down. Sensation, orientation, impulse, defensive response, pacing—that is already plenty to work with.

I go in a little and come back. Then again. The technical words are titration and pendulation, but the point is not to overwhelm myself while pretending that overwhelm is healing. Some people need three to six months to get good at staying with activation and returning to something steadier. Severe developmental trauma can take longer. I use those numbers for orientation, not as a calendar I have to obey. If somebody is already stable and has one clear target, I would add focused trauma work sooner.

Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga

Power yoga? No. Hot yoga? No. Somebody announcing, “Open your hips and release your trauma”? Also no. I mean the slow, choice-based kinds: trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, maybe very gentle yin with props, shorter holds, and an easy way out.

The teacher should keep giving the choice back:

“You might try…”
“You can keep your eyes open.”
“Come out of the shape whenever you want.”
“Is this useful, neutral, or too much?”
“There can be several right versions. Pick the one that fits your body.”

No intense stretching, breath retention, forced breathwork, aggressive hip opening, long passive holds, or hands-on adjustments without clear consent. And no pushing through because suffering is supposedly therapeutic. If I cannot leave the shape, change it, or say no, I come out.

Gentle Shaking / TRE

A little shaking can be enough. Basic TRE, a very light neurogenic tremor, standing and shaking gently, a short bit of playful movement. I am not trying to blast trauma out of my body. Compulsive, frightening, dissociative, hard to stop—any of those means I went too far.

The neck warning is literal. If the tremor reaches my neck while I am lying down, I do not let my unsupported head whip back and forth. I stop, make it smaller, support my head, or get an experienced provider to help support it. Then I get guidance before trying again. Forceful, rapid back-and-forth neck movement can injure the neck.
