## Use Childhood Photographs Gently
If picturing your little one feels fake, like you're just making it up, a real photo of you as a child can help, since there's no face or posture to invent. Yes, yes, it might feel a bit strange, seeing your own face from back then. But look at it gently and just notice what comes up.
While you look, notice what your body does and what feelings or urges show up. Is there something that younger you seems to want you to understand? Some of it you'll remember yourself, and some of it you might even glean from talking to family/friends from then ("You hated that coat"). It might just be an inference you're guessing from the picture, or perhaps it triggers an image or a dream. Or just nothing comes up. Ask what your little one needs from you now, and do a bit of it.
We don't need to ask it leading questions, like "Who abused me & how?" or "What memory is my body hiding?" Grilling one photo until a story shows up is a good way to get a story, but stories aren't necessarily the truth, and even if they are accurate, the feelings beneath them are still more important. Memory is an amorphous thing, easily influenced by guided imagery and suggestions, so we gotta be careful about that. Look at this study, for example, where people were shown a doctored photo of a childhood hot-air balloon ride they never took. Half of them actually started sorta remembering it. I'm sure you can relate.
A photo is just one moment that somebody picked and framed... not necessarily representative. Flipping thru an album may give different jolts in your body.
If you start to suspect someone of something serious, like abusing you, please don't confront or accuse anyone based only on what came up here. You might be looking at the photo thinking, "That would explain so much." Journal what came up and how it came up, and see if you can get support from someone level-headed who won't lead you toward an answer.
Put the photo back in its drawer when you're finished. If it starts tugging at you, so you keep going back to search it or you need to know for sure, or you're getting overwhelmed, take a break from it right then. Settle yourself in the present to close out properly. Re-evaluation counseling always ends on this "Present Time" reminder. Can be as simple and silly as "Oh, Today is Monday, my favorite day of the week!"
