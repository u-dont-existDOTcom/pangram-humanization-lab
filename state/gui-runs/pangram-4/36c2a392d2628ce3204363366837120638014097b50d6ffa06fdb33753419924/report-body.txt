### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.
Have you ever written a reply that you knew, even while typing it, would be a terrible idea to send? I have no objection to writing it. 😆 Just don't leave it where one angry thumb can publish it. Read it later. Some of it will probably make you cringe. Maybe one sentence won't. That's where the useful part might be hiding: the no, the request, or the apology you didn't want to make. The rest can die in Notes. Maybe all of it can.
It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.
If you say you'll come back later, come back.
Then read the version you didn't send. Not because it needs another edit. What was that person trying to stop? What did they need somebody to understand? Maybe they needed protection, reassurance, actual help. If the polished reply takes care of the conversation but you still turn against the person who wrote the first draft, you only finished one of the jobs.
