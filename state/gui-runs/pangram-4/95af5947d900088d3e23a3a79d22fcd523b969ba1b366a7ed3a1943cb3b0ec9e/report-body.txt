### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.
Start with the version you would not want screenshotted. Put it in Notes, write it on paper, whatever. Don't send it. When you read it later, some of the heat may look ridiculous and some of it may still be exactly what you mean. Keep the part that actually needs saying. If you owe them an apology, say so. If there's a request or a boundary in there, don't lose it just because the first draft was a mess. What actually needs to reach them may be two sentences, or nothing.
It doesn't mean you need to hide your upset, or apply for a permit to protest, but choose your battles and execute them wisely, so you don't end up regretting how you communicated later. As Lao Tsu might have said (if I could ever have read that whole boring Art of War book), a war with no casualties is the best possible war. When high IQ & EQ communication hits a writer's block, sometimes the best thing is to simply say, "I need some time to think about my response." And if the conflict really requires a response right now, then obviously just do your best rather than wait for the perfect timing.
If you tell them you'll come back later, actually come back.
After you've worked out what you're going to send, read the embarrassing first draft one more time. Who wrote that? What were they trying to get somebody to understand so badly? Maybe they needed protection, reassurance, or some actual help. You don't have to send the rant. But don't punish its author either. Sometimes the angry draft is where a completely reasonable protest showed up first.
