### Write It. Don't Send It Yet.
Keep writing the message. Just don't send it while you're still in that state. Let yourself write the embarrassing version; otherwise you can start editing yourself before you even know what you're trying to say.
Come back later and read it like somebody you care about handed it to you. Some lines may just be there to hurt the other person back. Those don't need to go anywhere. But maybe there's also a sentence in the middle that's basically, “No. I'm not okay with this.” Don't delete that just because it arrived surrounded by a rant. And if you actually did something wrong, the apology belongs there too.
What you send may be much shorter than what you wrote. Maybe it's just, “I need some time. I'll get back to you tomorrow.” If you say tomorrow, actually come back tomorrow. Or maybe you decide not to answer at all. If there's an actual safety issue, deal with that instead of sitting there polishing a text.
After you've figured out what to send, read the angry draft once more. What was its writer desperate to get heard about? What did they need right then—protection, reassurance, actual help? You can throw the message away without throwing that person away.
