Sometimes going back really does change something—you finally feel what you'd only been thinking about, or enough changes that you make a different choice afterward. Before I go looking for another round of that, though, I'd handle whatever actual problem is there and ask something more basic: what changed last time? If the honest answer is mostly "I still wasn't sure," and this is the hundredth check on whether I've finally grieved it correctly, I start wondering whether the healing itself has become another hook. The wound can still be completely real. Grief also comes back to familiar ground, which is why the return of the question doesn't settle whether the child has anything new to ask for.
Sometimes you have to carry that uncertainty back into the rest of your day. Let the thought be there while you go back to the person you were with or whatever you were doing. If your mind calls another meeting, you don't have to attend.
If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.
