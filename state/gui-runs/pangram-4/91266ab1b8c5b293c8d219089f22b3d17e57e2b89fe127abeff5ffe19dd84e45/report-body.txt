# Introduction
Trauma can be over and the body can still be doing it. You know you are safe, but you are bracing, scanning, going numb, collapsing, freezing, or suddenly flooding anyway. That is the part of trauma I am interested in here.
With complex or developmental trauma I usually do not begin by asking for the deepest memory. I want to know whether I can stay with what comes up without disappearing, whether I can stop, and whether I can be basically myself again afterward. If not, I would rather work on that than call overwhelm “deep processing.”
Sometimes that means regulation. Sometimes it means giving accumulated survival activation some way to move instead of trying to think your way out of a body that is still acting as if the danger is present. And sometimes there is enough stability to go straight to EMDR or another trauma-focused method. These are not steps in a program; they solve different problems at different times.
The inner-child connection is one reason I care about the body so much. Sometimes you can understand exactly what a younger part needs and still be unable to reach it because your whole system is clenched around the trauma. The body may need to become a little easier to inhabit before [inner-child therapy](http://innerchild.u-dont-exist.com/) stops feeling like being swallowed by the child-state.
## Your Physical State Can Change What Therapy Does
Therapy does not happen separately from whatever state the body is already in. Sleep, substances or medication, pain, nutrition, fear learning, inflammation, dissociation, glutamate/GABA balance, autonomic tone, and general physiological stability can all change what the same session does to you.
I have seen friends get very little from these therapies until they addressed physical neuroinflammation with things like low-dose naltrexone, phosphatidylserine, DMSO, and so on. In my own experience, I cannot do real healing work when I feel like I have toxins in my gut. Ozonated zeolite helps that a lot. Ozonated charcoal water also helps me when I am surrounded by chaotic energy fields, whether EMF or auric.
I think of that as neurological de-armoring: not another trauma technique, but part of the physical ground the techniques are working on. I have separate guides on [sleep](https://sleep.u-dont-exist.com/) and [neurological de-armoring](http://dearmor.u-dont-exist.com/) if that seems relevant to you.

# Where I Would Start
I do not have a preferred ladder. If one event is clearly the problem and you can stay present while touching it, I may go straight to EMDR. If touching trauma makes you disappear, flood, or stay wrecked for the rest of the day, I would rather use something gentler first.
Regulation can become avoidance too. I do not want someone spending forever “building capacity” because nobody is willing to treat the thing that is actually hurting them. I care about what happens in ordinary life: are you sleeping better, functioning better, relating better, getting freer? If sleep itself is the bottleneck, I have a separate [sleep guide](http://sleep.u-dont-exist.com/).
The intensity matters as much as the name of the method. Present-focused self-hypnosis is not the same thing as immersive memory work. A little shaking is not forced catharsis. Brainspotting can be used to resource and orient, or it can be used to enter material that is much hotter.
# Somatic Work and Inner-Child Reparenting
I do not do the gentler body work just to get calmer. It can make reparenting possible. But even relaxation is not the whole problem. I can access love and still have the younger part distrust it. If that child looks at the grown-up me and thinks, “yeah, that doesn’t look like things went well for us,” then a vow to protect and nurture can get a pretty reasonable response: “big fuckity whoopty doo—what are you going to do for me?” And I can make it worse by getting resentful at the younger version, as if the child were the one who was supposed to have figured out how to grow up. That is an internal war, not reparenting.
When I am so flooded that I am basically the child-state, there may not be much adult perspective available anyway. In my inner-child work I call the functions I need the Nurturer and Protector. The Protector becomes more credible when it actually does something in ordinary life; even one real protective action can matter. If those adult voices do not feel available yet, a neutral witness is enough to begin, or I can use what I call borrowed adulthood: take a sentence, boundary, or direction from somebody safe while I carry a little of it myself. Gentle [self-hypnosis](http://hypnosis.u-dont-exist.com/) can help too when it stays present-focused instead of pulling you deeply into memories or child dialogue.
Sometimes the body itself is the bottleneck. If my solar plexus is tight or there is too much emotional tension to access heart chakra love, EFT, Somatic Experiencing, trauma-sensitive yoga, gentle shaking or qigong, non-forced longer exhales, or gentle Buteyko-style breathing can help the body soften. These can prepare for inner-child work or continue alongside it.
Brainspotting can sit inside the reparenting work too. A resource-oriented or carefully titrated session is very different from using it to enter highly charged developmental material. With diffuse developmental or relational trauma, I am more inclined to use EMDR after there is at least some access to a Nurturer and Protector who can help hold what the memory work opens. That is not a universal rule; a stable person with one discrete event may be ready much earlier.
Once I can actually feel love, I do not have to use the opening to go looking for pain. I can bring to mind a pet, a baby, a friend, a partner, or anyone I naturally love. For the simple loop I use in my inner-child work, one hand goes on the center of my chest at the heart chakra and the other on the solar plexus, where I feel the vulnerable younger part. The solar plexus is a physically vulnerable part of the torso—one slap there hurts quite a lot—so it feels natural to me as a place to feel the vulnerable child. Then I imagine sending love from the heart to the child, receiving love back from the child, and sending it up again in an infinite beautiful loop.
I would save deep memory work, immersive child dialogue, deep hypnosis, and suggestive exploration for when you can stay present, stop voluntarily, and come back afterward.

# Building Enough Safety to Stay Present
## Somatic Experiencing
[Somatic Experiencing](http://traumahealing.org/) is one of the things I would reach for when even getting near the material sends the body over the edge. It works with sensation, orientation, impulse, defensive response, and pacing without requiring the whole story first.
Titration and pendulation are useful because you are not trying to win a contest with the trauma. You touch a tolerable amount of activation, notice what happens, then come back toward something steadier. Some people spend three to six months building that capacity, and severe developmental trauma can take longer. I would treat that as orientation, not a countdown. If you are already stable and have a clear target, there may be no reason to wait that long before adding trauma-focused work.

## Trauma-Sensitive / Restorative Yoga
When I say yoga here, I do not mean power yoga, hot yoga, or somebody announcing, “Open your hips and release your trauma.” I mean slow, choice-based work: trauma-sensitive yoga, restorative yoga, gentle somatic yoga, or very gentle yin. With yin I would keep it propped, short, and easy to leave.
Good trauma-sensitive instructions sound like:
- “You might try…”
- “You can keep your eyes open.”
- “You can come out of the shape at any time.”
- “Notice whether this feels useful, neutral, or too much.”
- “A posture can have several correct versions; choose one that fits your body.”
What I do not want is intense stretching, forced breathwork, breath retention, aggressive hip openers, long passive holds, hands-on adjustments without clear consent, or the idea that you should push through sensation because suffering through it is somehow therapeutic. The whole point here is that choice stays available.
## Gentle Shaking / TRE
At this point I am talking about a little shaking, not trying to blast trauma out of the body. Basic [TRE](https://traumaprevention.com/frequently-asked-questions/), very light neurogenic tremoring, gentle standing shaking, or short playful movement can be enough. If it becomes compulsive, frightening, dissociative, or hard to stop, it has gone past what I mean by gentle.
