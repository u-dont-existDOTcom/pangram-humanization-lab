So the adult comes back and sees what the kid wrote. Maybe it's too sharp, maybe it's unfair, maybe it's exactly what he feels. He doesn't say, "Jesus, give me that." He says, "Okay. I get why you want to say it like that." Then he sits there for a second and asks, "What are you actually trying to make them understand?" The kid might still want the mean sentence. Fine. Leave it there for now. Write the next one underneath it. Sometimes once the hurt part knows it isn't being shut up, it doesn't have to fight so hard to get every word onto the page. Then the two of you can start changing a few lines—not to make them nicer, necessarily, but to make them say what you actually mean.
