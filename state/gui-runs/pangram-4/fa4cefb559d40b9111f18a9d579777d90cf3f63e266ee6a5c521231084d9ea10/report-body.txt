Then reread the first draft and picture the kid sitting there with the pencil, and before correcting anything, just look at what he actually wrote. Don’t snatch the page away because he used the wrong words, or make him copy out some nicer sentence that no longer sounds like him; sit down and help him with it. He may have written something unfair, or the problem may only be that it came out badly, and either way he can still be angry for a perfectly good reason. The reply needs to work for whoever receives it, obviously, but it also has to remain something the person who wrote that first version was trying to say, which may take a little more looking at before you know what to keep.

Advanced AI detection tools don’t treat a document as a single giant block of text. Rather, these tools break down the text into segments, sentences, and paragraphs. If you are using an AI detector and a particular document gets a low score (e.g., 30% AI-generated), this usually indicates a hybrid document.
