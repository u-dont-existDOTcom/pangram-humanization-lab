After the hundredth check—have I finally grieved this correctly?—I start wondering whether the healing itself has become another hook. None of that means the wound wasn't real.
If there's something real-world to deal with, do that before turning it into more inner-child work. Then—actually, before doing another round at all—I'd look back at the last one. Did anything change? Sometimes the return is useful—you finally feel what you'd only been thinking about, or enough clicks that you make a different choice. I've got no problem with that. What worries me is when the question is back mostly because the last answer didn't feel certain enough. Grief revisits old ground anyway, which is why I don't think the question coming back, by itself, tells you the child has a new request.
If you think you're checking, maybe don't turn that into another question you have to solve. Let it sit there. Go back to the person you were with or whatever you were doing. If the thought follows you, it follows you. The child can still matter without getting called into another meeting every time your mind says something.
If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.
