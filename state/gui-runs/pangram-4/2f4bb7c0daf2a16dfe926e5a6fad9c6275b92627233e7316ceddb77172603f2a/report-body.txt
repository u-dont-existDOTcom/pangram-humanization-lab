The wound can still be completely real; that's not what I'm questioning. After the hundredth "have I finally grieved this correctly?" I start wondering whether healing itself has become another hook.
If there's an actual safety issue or practical problem, deal with that first.
Before another round, I'd look back at the last one and ask what changed—whether something you'd only been thinking about finally became a feeling, or whether enough clicked that you actually did something differently afterward—because if all that came back was the same question and the same uncertainty, I don't know that another round is what the child is asking for. Grief comes back to familiar ground, though, so even that isn't a perfect test.
If it looks more like checking, let the question stay open and go back to whatever you were doing. It can follow you. Caring for the child doesn't require holding another meeting every time your mind produces a sentence.
If these exercises start feeling like a ritual you have to complete before you're allowed to live, stop there.
