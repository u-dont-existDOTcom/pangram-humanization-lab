# Owner-language handoff context incident — 2026-08-22

Status: transferable workflow correction from direct owner feedback during Romance detector repair.

## Incident

After the Romance Part-1 repair exhausted the legitimate assistant/local-rewrite lanes, the worker correctly stopped and requested fresh owner language. The request named a residual as `Talk — 112 words` and summarized abstract functions such as libido mismatch, resentment, naked honesty, and bodily fit.

Joel could not tell what section he was being asked to write. His response was: `that wasn't enough info for me to understand the section i'm writing. Talk? about making love? about trucks? i'm assuming it's the before making love talk`.

Once the worker clarified that the destination was **`Talk about making love before you do it`**, specifically the sexual-compatibility/libido-mismatch tail after the meaning-of-sex discussion and before the naked-honesty/bodies-fitting paragraphs, Joel immediately supplied concrete natural owner prose with a remembered Christian-magazine example, a lived relationship example, and the ongoing-communication consequence.

## Finding

A narrow owner-language request is not actually narrow or usable merely because it lists abstract semantic functions. The owner needs enough **reader-facing placement context** to reconstruct the thought naturally.

When asking an owner to supply fresh language after a detector/editing stop, provide:

1. the exact article and section heading;
2. a plain-language description of where inside the section the requested passage sits;
3. what the immediately preceding paragraph has just established;
4. what the immediately following paragraph will do;
5. the specific thought/job being replaced or supplied;
6. which neighboring functions are **not** being replaced;
7. optionally the exact old paragraph when seeing it is useful and does not anchor the owner into copying model syntax.

Do not rely on internal labels such as `Talk`, `window 3`, `112 words`, `PU-TALK-09`, or detector coordinates as if they were meaningful writing context to the owner.

## Operational rule

Before requesting fresh owner language, translate the internal preservation/detector state back into the article's **reader-visible thought sequence**. The owner should be able to answer without opening GitHub, remembering the detector history, or guessing what `Talk` refers to.

This is compatible with minimum-dose editing: richer context does not authorize broader changes. It simply makes the requested owner contribution intelligible.

## Scope

This is a workflow/elicitation lesson, not detector evidence and not a claim that more context always produces detector-Human prose. It applies to owner-language recovery in article editing, reconstruction, detector repair, and similar authorial handoffs.
