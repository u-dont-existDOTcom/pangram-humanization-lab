## Hard ban

Do not generate the formulaic cadence:

> affirmative fragment/sentence. Not really / not quite / not exactly / not merely + reversal or qualification.

This includes close variants whose rhetorical job is the same: state a neat affirmative beat, stop, then manufacture a miniature corrective or antithetical beat in a second clipped sentence.

The ban is on the **construction and cadence**, not on the individual words `not`, `really`, `quite`, or `exactly` when they are naturally required elsewhere.

This extends the existing Joel ban on `That's not X. It's Y.` / negative-reveal constructions. Both forms package one ordinary relationship between thoughts as a polished two-beat contrast.

## Preferred repair principle

State the actual relation between the thoughts in ordinary syntax rather than staging a reversal.

Joel's own repair of the triggering line:

> It was a good tip, but wasn't meant to fill a whole curriculum.

That sentence is an example of the principle, not a mandatory template. Many other natural realizations are possible. Do not mechanically replace every banned construction with `X, but Y`; choose syntax that fits the actual causal, concessive, temporal, or qualifying relation.
