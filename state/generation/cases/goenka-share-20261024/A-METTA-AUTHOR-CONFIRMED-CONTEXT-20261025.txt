In the Metta Sutta, you wish ease and happiness to beings, yourself included. Metta calms the hindrances and builds goodwill. With metta, you can wish for suffering to end without making it your job to end it. Try to force it, and even that wish can turn desperate—or leave you afraid you'll fail.
Theravada and most Mahayana Buddhist traditions treat metta as essential groundwork before the deeper work of unweaving the sense streams that make up the mind.
