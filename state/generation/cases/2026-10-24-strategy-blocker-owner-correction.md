## What actually changed

- **Live question:** GPT asked to “look at how those weeks actually went,” a generic introspective step. Joel asks what is *in the way* and makes the object of the feared discovery “what's underneath this,” preserving the deeper psychological question instead of method efficacy.
- **Case logic:** Joel cleanly distinguishes three *possible* barriers: the strategy not meaningfully implemented, a practical environment without alone time, and fear/a protective response to going deeper. GPT's “It's easy to...” commentary muddied action with perceived action and jumped through a model-style list of reassuring alternatives.
- **Experiencer and agency:** “it felt scary” and “like some part of you was trying to protect you from going deeper” report an experience and its possible protective function; “fear got there first” is a familiar personifying shortcut and leaves the mechanism unspoken.
- **Directness:** “look for what exactly is in the way” supplies a clear action instead of “I'd look at...” narrator distance. The repeated “Or” is meaningful because the cases are causally distinct. Do not ban it as a word pattern.
- **Authorial revision:** Joel *removed* the original claim that reading and preparing can *feel* like doing the work (including the article's self-reference) and replaced it with a more direct non-implementation case; “played around with lightly” is new.
