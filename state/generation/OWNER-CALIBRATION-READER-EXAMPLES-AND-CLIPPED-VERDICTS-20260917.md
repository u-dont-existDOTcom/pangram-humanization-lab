# Owner calibration — reader examples, owner-example flags, and clipped verdict bans

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: **ACTIVE OWNER-CALIBRATED GENERATION LESSON**

## Direct owner correction

Joel clarified two additional ways to avoid turning humanization into optimized instruction-manual prose.

### 1. Real-life movement does not require invented autobiography

Do **not** fabricate Joel memories or first-person experiences. But a writer may use ordinary, source-grounded reader situations and ask whether the reader recognizes something similar in their own life.

For example, when the source thought is about apologizing for one real mistake while abandoning the rest of one's position, the prose may ask the reader whether they have ever apologized for one part of an argument and then noticed the whole original point disappearing because the other person was still upset. The example must remain within the supplied meaning; it is not authority to add new psychological claims or invented specifics.

This is useful because real social situations can create natural thought movement without converting every source function into one maximally efficient explanatory sentence.

### 2. Flag places where Joel's own real example would materially help

When a subsection would become substantially more grounded through an actual Joel experience, mark that opportunity for the supervisor/owner rather than inventing it. Do not make owner autobiography a mandatory prerequisite when a source-grounded reader example can do the job adequately.

Useful owner-example flags should be specific about the missing function, such as:
- a time Joel really owed repair for one part of a conflict but still needed to hold onto another part of his position;
- a time he kept explaining because he wanted the other person to agree or understand before he felt allowed to act;
- a time one ugly conversation initially looked diagnostic but later behavior changed his judgment.

These are example slots, not claims that such events occurred.

## Standalone clipped verdict ban for generated humanization

Direct owner rule: in fresh model-generated humanization prose, do not use the clipped standalone interjections:

- `Great.`
- `Fine.`
- `Good.`

Joel's reason is not that these words are impossible for humans. They have become extremely frequent AI compression devices: a whole evaluation or transition is collapsed into the shortest possible sentence and the prose immediately marches onward.

This ban applies to fresh humanization generation. It does **not** authorize rewriting owner-authored or locked prose merely because it contains one of these words, and it does not ban the words when used normally inside a longer sentence.

Do not silently expand the ban to `Okay.` or other words without owner evidence.

## Relationship to predictable marching advancement

This correction extends `OWNER-CALIBRATION-MARCHING-EFFICIENCY-20260917.md`.

The problem remains sustained maximum semantic efficiency: every sentence does one crisp job, advances the argument, and then gets out of the way. Reader examples and genuine owner examples are useful not because anecdotes are inherently Human, but because they can restore actual social/lived movement that a function-ledger realization compresses away.

Do not repair marching prose by padding. A generic invented anecdote, decorative rhetorical question, random aside, or unnecessary repetition is not a valid substitute for a real thought route.
