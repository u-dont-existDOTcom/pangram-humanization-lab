T01 — fake personal stake / irrelevant first-person authority
A general criterion, standard, boundary, consequence, or recommendation is routed through the author's first-person want, need, preference, care, or entitlement, so the reader is asked to care what the author wants/gets to do instead of being given the point directly. This can happen even in autobiography.
Decision test:
1. Find first-person want/need/preference/care/permission/entitlement framing.
2. Ask whether its content is really a general criterion about what a process, helper, relationship, practice, or rule should allow, avoid, produce, or imply.
3. Depersonalize it into a direct general claim. If that loses no author-specific observation, actual reaction/disposition, event, real personal preference as subject, real question, or authority-limiting function, mark PRESENT.
A practical-necessity claim backed by a mechanism, or genuine autobiographical experience, is not this tell merely because it uses "I".

T02 — cumulative instruction-manual / compressed-listicle cadence
At paragraph/section scale, consecutive compressed commands, verdicts, questions, conditions, distinctions, or lessons each do one clean teaching job and hand off to the next. A list, imperative, direct advice, or question alone is not this tell.

T03 — abrupt complication without reader-visible setup

T04 — scene-skinned semantic staircase
A concrete/coherent scene merely skins the same source-function checklist: each scene beat maps one-for-one to a protected obligation in source-ledger order. Judge the observable topology; absence of the hidden source ledger is not by itself a reason for UNCERTAIN.

T05 — synthetic/didactic prop continuity

T06 — generic therapeutic abstraction
Generic therapy-safe abstraction appears where the prose sounds concrete, e.g. vague grief/old thing/healing/process language that summarizes instead of carrying a specific lived relation.

T07 — simulated spontaneity / fake filler as transition camouflage

T09 — equalized / optimal semantic efficiency

T10 — generic permission syntax
Permission or relief packaging carries a point as a reusable counseling formula rather than as a relation whose logic depends on this passage.
Gate examples include "you don't have to", "you don't need to", "there's no need to", "it's okay to", "you're allowed to", "don't feel you must", "don't feel pressure to", "don't worry about", "no rush to", or close variants. A prohibition/warning against an action is not permission packaging merely because it starts with "don't".
Decision test:
1. Portability: after resolving pronouns, could the sentence's logic work as competent counseling about a different personal/relational/emotional problem by swapping topic nouns?
2. Local anchor: would that move break the logic because the sentence sets a specific exception/order for an earlier claim, depends on a particular fact/mechanism/observation established here, or carries situation-dependent irony/joke?
PRESENT if portable and not locally anchored. Otherwise ABSENT.
Topic nouns, back-references, a named addressee, sincere belief, idiosyncratic diction, or a generic reassurance clause do not by themselves create a local anchor.

T11 — generic bridge / connective tissue
A transition mainly announces, justifies, or connects document functions rather than changing the situation the live thought is about.
Decision test:
1. Does the unit mainly connect/announce/justify moving from one part to another?
2. Does it supply a subject-matter state change the following material needs and does not itself make clear: a new time/phase, case/person, premise/condition, or causal state of the people/things discussed?
3. Deletion test: if removed, would the following material still follow from the preceding material, losing only announcement/relevance/linkage?
PRESENT when there is no needed subject-matter state change and deletion costs only connective/relevance packaging. ABSENT when the transition supplies a needed state change or deletion harms coherence.
A sentence can explicitly say X is why Y matters and still be generic linkage if the relation is between document functions rather than the subject itself. Bare connectors belong to T03/T07 as appropriate, not T11.

T12 — symmetry / balanced contrast / tidy taxonomy
