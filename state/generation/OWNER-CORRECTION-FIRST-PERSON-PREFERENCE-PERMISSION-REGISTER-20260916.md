# Owner correction — first-person preference/permission wrappers in advice prose

Date: 2026-09-16

Status: **REUSABLE GENERATION/EDITORIAL LESSON; not a Pangram phrase law**

## Owner correction

Joel objected to constructions such as:

- `I want therapy to ...`
- `I don't actually get to ...`

when the sentence is not genuinely about Joel's personal preference or permission, but is instead trying to state a general criterion, boundary, consequence, or recommendation.

His reason: the framing sounds strange because the reader has no reason to care what the author *wants* or what the author *gets to do* when the underlying claim is supposed to apply directly to the situation.

## Generation rule

When the underlying thought is general advice or a criterion, state that criterion directly.

Examples of the repair direction:

- Instead of `I want therapy to leave me more able to stand on my own feet`, state directly that a good therapeutic process should make the helper less necessary over time.
- Instead of `If every no becomes resistance, I don't actually get to say no`, state directly that treating every disagreement as resistance makes disagreement impossible or folds every answer back into the same theory.

This is the same family of reader-facing register problem already seen in significance-staging first-person wrappers: the author inserts personal desire/permission where the real sentence job is to make the claim itself.

## Limits

This is **not** a ban on first person, `want`, `need`, or permission language.

Keep first person when:

- Joel is recounting autobiography or direct experience;
- Joel's actual preference is itself the subject;
- a remembered thought or quotation requires it;
- the point is specifically about what Joel wanted, needed, believed, chose, or was allowed to do.

Source-derived statements such as a child learning that what they wanted did not matter are unaffected.

Do not turn this into a detector charm or literal word blacklist. The issue is the mismatch between sentence job and rhetorical wrapper.

## Current application

Inner Child Therapy Episode 007 R7 removes the `I want ...` / `I get to ...` / `I don't get to ...` wrappers from the general helper/autonomy advice while preserving the underlying helper-fallibility, disagreement, anti-dependency, and adult-role-return functions.
