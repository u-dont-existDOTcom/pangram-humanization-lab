# Joel-specific Human-facing tell construction library — v1

Date: 2026-09-20
Status: **FAILED AS A DIRECT GENERATIVE RECIPE / RETAIN AS DIAGNOSTIC-RETRIEVAL CATALOG ONLY**
Purpose: preserve Human-facing patterns as diagnostic/retrieval hypotheses. Do not use them as simultaneous composition constraints.

## Core boundary

These are **Human-facing realization patterns in Joel's byline work**, supported by natural-owner prose, owner-final prose, aligned owner corrections, successful supervised revisions, or controlled evidence.

They are not:
- universal claims about how humans write;
- proof of human authorship;
- Pangram-causal phrase rules;
- permission to fabricate memories, hesitation, slang, mistakes, asides, or details;
- a menu from which every paragraph must pick several items.

A Human-facing tell is usable only when the **source thought naturally supports it**. The same visible form can be fake/model-shaped when inserted merely to look Human.

## 2026-09-20 failure amendment

The first direct use of this library as a generation recipe failed badly on the Inner Child checking section. The selected H2 candidate was owner-reported **Pangram AI High** and the owner said he could not even understand it.

Causal finding: these patterns were descriptive/diagnostic evidence, not validated model-generation controls. Applying several of them simultaneously caused the writer to optimize against the critic's taxonomy, hide preservation-ledger structure through clause packing, invent synthetic first-person/colloquial voice, and degrade readability.

Therefore the library is **not** an active writer checklist. Until prospective transfer evidence exists:
- retrieve it only after generation to diagnose a literal candidate, or to help select a small number of relevant owner examples;
- do not expose HT01–HT14 as simultaneous composition requirements;
- do not treat T1/T2 owner evidence as proof of model transfer skill;
- do not use the preservation ledger as a prose outline;
- basic reader comprehension blocks all detector/humanization optimization.

For fresh composition, follow the generator-learning compiler: writer sees the live thought, mesoscale role, and a small literal positive calibration set; preservation and tell diagnostics come afterward.

### T1 — strong positive construction evidence
Supported by natural owner / owner-final prose plus direct owner calibration across more than one passage or source family.

### T2 — strong aligned correction evidence
Supported by a clean or near-clean model-before -> owner-after same-thought transformation, owner-final correction, or supervised successful revision. Useful for construction, but narrower than T1.

### T3 — detector/controlled interaction evidence
Useful diagnostically, but not by itself a positive imitation target. A Pangram-Human model sentence can still look AI to Joel.

### T4 — hypothesis only
Interesting but not yet promoted into the active construction library.

Only T1/T2 patterns below are positive construction tells. T3 material appears only as constraints/counterexamples.

# Positive construction tells

## HT01 — Unequal thought duration

**Evidence:** T1.

**Human-facing pattern:** one concern genuinely receives more clauses, sentences, or paragraph space than another. Side conditions can remain brief or subordinate.

This is visible in natural owner material and in Joel's direct objection to predictable marching advancement: model prose tends to allocate one crisp sentence per protected function.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify the actual pressure center of the paragraph/section.
2. Let that concern occupy the amount of thought it really needs.
3. Compress or subordinate secondary obligations rather than giving every source unit its own sentence.
4. Stop when the live concern has reached its natural point; do not equalize paragraph lengths afterward.

**Valid when:** the source has unequal importance, uncertainty, emotional pressure, causal weight, or practical consequence.

**Invalid simulation:** arbitrarily making one sentence long, padding with commentary, or shortening another sentence solely for rhythm.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Give X more room because it is the live problem. Fold Y into that movement or leave it subordinate; do not give X and Y matching sentence weight.

---

## HT02 — Stay inside the thought before abstracting it

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** prose remains for another beat with what was noticed, felt, asked, or changed before converting it into a compact concept or lesson.

Owner calibration repeatedly favors passages where the cognition unfolds before the label. The reverse form—event -> immediate polished synthesis—is a frequent AI-shaped failure.

**Construction operation:**
1. Locate the first abstract summary/label.
2. Ask what the person actually notices immediately before that summary.
3. Realize that local perception, reaction, or question first.
4. Name the concept only if the reader still needs it.
5. Do not append a second sentence explaining what the concrete beat demonstrated.

**Valid when:** the source includes a perceptual change, contradiction, failed attempt, reaction, question, or discovery route.

**Invalid simulation:** fake hesitation, filler like `I guess`, random self-correction, or invented autobiographical detail.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Delete the abstract topic sentence as the entry point. Start from the actual change in perception/action; let the concept arrive later if it is still needed.

---

## HT03 — Preserve a real unresolved tension

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** two source-grounded truths remain in tension without the paragraph immediately reconciling them into a balanced formula.

Examples in the calibration corpus include resentment coexisting with care, real hurt coexisting with possible checking, and an apology coexisting with a still-valid complaint.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify what the source genuinely does not resolve yet.
2. State or enact both pressures without giving them matched rhetorical halves.
3. Allow the tension to survive into the next sentence/paragraph or into action.
4. Conclude only when the source itself supplies a resolution.

**Valid when:** the source genuinely leaves causal, emotional, moral, practical, or epistemic uncertainty open.

**Invalid simulation:** `maybe A / maybe B` symmetry, generic ambiguity, artificial coyness, or refusing to state a conclusion that the source actually establishes.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Keep X true without using it to cancel Y. Do not reconcile them here; let the next move happen with both still active.

---

## HT04 — Consequential interruption

**Evidence:** T1.

**Human-facing pattern:** a real-world priority interrupts the conceptual line because it actually changes what has to happen next.

A strong current example is actual danger/practical necessity interrupting further introspection. This differs from a polished transition because the interruption changes priority rather than smoothing prose.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify the source condition that outranks the current intellectual/therapeutic line.
2. Interrupt at the point where it becomes relevant.
3. State the required action or consequence.
4. Return to the prior line only if the source naturally resumes it.

**Valid when:** safety, consent, timing, another person's action, a concrete decision, or another real condition genuinely changes the next move.

**Invalid simulation:** inserting `But first...` merely for cadence or using a random practical aside to break a staircase.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Let the real-world condition interrupt the analysis here. State what it makes the person do before resuming the inner question.

---

## HT05 — Authorial judgment with consequence

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** the writer owns a judgment and that judgment changes the next action, interpretation, or boundary.

This is different from wrapping a neutral synthesis in `I think`.

**Construction operation:**
1. Find the source's actual judgment, preference, refusal, doubt, or decision.
2. State it in ordinary first-person only if it really belongs to Joel's stance.
3. Let it alter what happens next.
4. Do not follow it with a generic explanation of why having a judgment is reasonable.

**Valid when:** owner/source material supplies the judgment.

**Invalid simulation:** adding `I think`, `I wonder`, or `for me` to an otherwise generic explanatory sentence.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Put Joel's actual judgment at the decision point and let it change the treatment of the next case; do not turn it into a neutral rule with a first-person wrapper.

---

## HT06 — One lived/social move carries several functions

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** one question, exchange, example, admission, reaction, or action naturally carries multiple semantic obligations instead of one sentence being assigned to each obligation.

**Construction operation:**
1. List the protected functions in the local span.
2. Ask whether one real social/cognitive move already contains several of them.
3. Build around that move.
4. Delete explanatory sentences whose only job is to unpack implications the move already makes available.

**Valid when:** the source supplies or licenses a real interaction, question, decision, recognizable reader situation, or source-grounded image.

**Invalid simulation:** inventing a story, dialogue, or rhetorical question solely to combine functions.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Let the actual exchange/question carry X + Y + Z. Remove the separate explanation of each unless one remains necessary afterward.

---

## HT07 — Chronology/perception instead of a balanced taxonomy

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** events or recognitions unfold in an actual sequence, with one branch becoming dominant, instead of being presented as two or three matched categories.

Episode 007's protective-part -> reach child -> become child repair is a strong aligned example.

**Construction operation:**
1. Find hidden matched branches in the current sentence.
2. Recover the actual order in which one thing leads to another.
3. Tell that sequence.
4. Let minor alternatives remain subordinate instead of giving them equal syntactic status.

**Valid when:** source chronology or attentional sequence is known.

**Invalid simulation:** inventing chronology where the source only gives alternatives, or simply replacing bullets with `then`.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Replace the A/B taxonomy with the actual sequence: first X happens; if/when that gives way, Y becomes the dominant problem. Keep the secondary branch subordinate.

---

## HT08 — Asymmetric concession and complication

**Evidence:** T1.

**Human-facing pattern:** a real benefit or concession can be acknowledged briefly while the complication receives disproportionate space because it is the actual subject.

The natural 5-MeO guide supplies a clear owner-confirmed example; Joel's calibration rejects polished balanced pro/con packaging.

**Construction operation:**
1. Preserve the concession exactly to its required scope.
2. Do not mirror it with an equally polished opposing half.
3. Move into the complication and let it consume the needed space.
4. Do not summarize the fair-mindedness of the result afterward.

**Valid when:** both benefit and complication are real but not equally important to the local argument.

**Invalid simulation:** one-token `sure` concession used rhetorically, or exaggerating the complication merely to create asymmetry.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Keep the real benefit in one brief beat. Let the complication take over because it is the live issue; do not build a symmetric pro/con pair.

---

## HT09 — Earned recurrence

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** a question, image, phrase, or concern returns because the underlying situation genuinely recurs and the repetition changes or accumulates meaning.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify an existing source-grounded recurring question/image.
2. Reuse it only at a point where the situation actually returns.
3. Let recurrence itself show persistence/escalation.
4. Avoid translating the recurrence into an immediate conceptual summary.

**Valid when:** recurrence is part of the source thought or actual case.

**Invalid simulation:** mimetic repetition (`Sometimes -> then -> again -> takeaway`) engineered solely for texture.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Bring the same question back when the checking/interaction genuinely recurs. Let the recurrence do the work; do not append a sentence explaining that it is repetitive.

---

## HT10 — Let a source-grounded metaphor/image remain partly untranslated

**Evidence:** T1/T2.

**Human-facing pattern:** a real metaphor or image carries several relations at once, and prose continues from what it makes possible instead of decoding every component into therapist/editor language.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify the source-grounded image/metaphor.
2. Determine which protected functions it already conveys.
3. Keep those functions inside the image.
4. Add explanation only for a dependency the reader could not otherwise follow.

**Valid when:** the image is owner/source-derived and genuinely load-bearing.

**Invalid simulation:** generating a fresh cute metaphor, catchphrase, or visual flourish to look Human.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Keep the image doing X/Y/Z. Remove the sentence that translates those same functions back into abstract prose.

---

## HT11 — Necessary transition rather than generic connective tissue

**Evidence:** T1 plus controlled counterexample evidence.

**Human-facing pattern:** a bridge is earned because it changes time, case, premise, speaker, or causal state. It is not merely a sentence saying how two paragraphs relate.

The Romance reciprocity work showed that deleting a real temporal bridge hurt coherence even while explanatory aftercare was removable.

**Construction operation:**
1. Ask what changes across the boundary.
2. If the reader would otherwise misread the time/case/premise, state that change plainly.
3. Do not explain the thematic relationship unless the relationship itself is new information.

**Valid when:** a genuine dependency changes.

**Invalid simulation:** `This is why...`, `The same principle...`, `That experience is why...` when the only job is editorial linkage.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Keep a bridge only for the actual change from X to Y. Name the change itself, not the fact that the paragraphs are connected.

---

## HT12 — Source-grounded reader recognition

**Evidence:** T1 owner calibration.

**Human-facing pattern:** prose moves socially toward the reader through a recognizable situation or question grounded in the supplied meaning, without inventing Joel autobiography.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify a concrete social/practical configuration already entailed by the source.
2. Phrase it as a recognizable reader situation or a genuine question when useful.
3. Let the scenario expose the problem before abstracting it.
4. Keep all details inside the supplied meaning.

**Valid when:** the source supports the scenario and it materially clarifies the live pressure.

**Invalid simulation:** generic `Have you ever...?` hooks, fabricated specifics, or reader anecdotes added only for warmth.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> Put the reader inside the source-supported situation first; ask the question that naturally arises there instead of stating the abstract capability/problem up front.

---

## HT13 — Earned short stop

**Evidence:** T1.

**Human-facing pattern:** a short sentence or paragraph ends the local movement because the practical boundary, joke, refusal, permission, fact, or consequence is sufficient. No mini-conclusion follows.

Natural owner sources contain many short direct stops beside much longer developments.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify the real stopping event.
2. State it.
3. Stop.
4. Let the next paragraph/heading supply any later development.

**Valid when:** the local function is actually complete even though broader implications remain open.

**Invalid simulation:** clipped `Great.` / `Fine.` / `Good.`, slogan endings, or arbitrary fragmentation.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> End on the actual boundary/consequence. Delete the sentence that restates what the reader should learn from it.

---

## HT14 — Social-act / register change inside a section

**Evidence:** T1.

**Human-facing pattern:** the writer's relation to the reader can change naturally—observation -> question -> admission -> warning -> direct counsel—when the underlying thought demands it.

Positive owner references such as Catch the Hook and long Romance sections do not maintain one uniform expository act.

**Construction operation:**
1. Identify whether the source function changes the writer-reader relation.
2. Let the prose change act at that point.
3. Keep neighboring acts unequal in duration.
4. Do not force a regular alternation.

**Valid when:** the source genuinely moves from describing to asking, owning, warning, inviting, etc.

**Invalid simulation:** adding rhetorical questions, jokes, or direct address on a schedule.

**Repair-ledger instruction form:**
> This is where the prose stops explaining and starts [asking/warning/admitting/advising]. Make that act change directly; do not add a transition announcing it.

# Important non-tells / counterexamples

The following are **not** Human tells by themselves:

- first person;
- contractions;
- fragments;
- long sentences;
- short sentences;
- rhetorical questions;
- direct advice;
- examples;
- lists;
- colloquial words;
- sentence-length variation;
- paragraph-length variation;
- em dashes or lack of em dashes;
- `maybe`;
- repetition;
- imperatives;
- source citations;
- metaphors.

All of these can occur in Human or model prose. They become useful only when they instantiate one of the source-earned relations above.

Likewise, Pangram-Human status alone does not promote a realization into this library. Joel has explicitly judged several detector-Human model passages as visibly AI-shaped because they still march efficiently.

# Construction selection gate

Before assigning a Human-facing tell to a red span, answer:

1. **What protected function must survive?**
2. **What is the paragraph/section role in the mesoscale map?**
3. **Which HT relation is already latent in the source?**
4. **What evidence makes that relation real rather than decorative?**
5. **What current AI-shaped operation is suppressing it?**
6. **What is the smallest structural instruction that restores it?**

If #3/#4 has no answer, do **not** manufacture a Human tell. Use a different repair or preserve the span until stronger source/owner evidence exists.

# Example application pattern

Bad repair request:
> Make this sound more human and less polished.

Better repair ledger:
> **Disposition: merge/rewrite.** The current two sentences create a matched useful-vs-checking taxonomy. Preserve both possibilities, but the checking branch is the actual pressure center. Use HT01 + HT03: let the checking uncertainty occupy more thought, keep the real-wound/grief complication unresolved, and fold the useful-return case into a brief subordinate beat. Do not end with a sentence summarizing the distinction.

Chat then executes that instruction against the literal source.

# Promotion discipline

A new proposed tell does not enter the positive library merely because:
- one owner rewrite used it;
- one Pangram score improved;
- one cold audit preferred it;
- it sounds less model-like in isolation.

Promotion requires:
1. exact source/provenance;
2. a positive construction operation, not just a prohibition;
3. at least one natural-owner/owner-final or aligned owner example;
4. counterexample check;
5. scope statement;
6. where feasible, transfer evidence on a different rhetorical target.

Two successful fresh transfers on different targets are required before calling the corresponding generator behavior a **production skill** under the generator-learning compiler.
