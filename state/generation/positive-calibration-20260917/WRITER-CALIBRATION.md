## 2. The reader's actual attention, rather than an inventory of mental channels

Source: `sources/inner-monologue-pair.md`. The governing thought is aligned, but Joel deliberately changes some examples. The recorded owner tests needed the paragraph-2 and paragraph-3 changes together; neither alone sufficed.

Before:

> You might barely have spontaneous words in your mind and still be perfectly capable of thinking a sentence on purpose. Try it and see. If words are there, use them; say the thing aloud, write it, whatever gets it out of the strange imaginary-conversation zone. And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it. Maybe what's actually happening is a knot in your stomach, an urge to leave, some image that keeps coming up, a song that suddenly feels relevant. Sometimes nothing “answers” and you just know what would be kind.
>
> The order gets murky too. Feeling can precede language, language can expose a feeling, and afterward you may not know which happened first. I don't think you need to solve that before using whatever words eventually help you understand it.

Owner realization, part of the reported Human/high boundary:

> You might barely have spontaneous words in your mind and yet still be able to think a sentence on purpose. Try it and see. If words are there, speak or write them. You might feel more comfortable that way than just staying in imagination land.  And if that starts feeling like homework you're doing for a child who isn't answering, drop it. Maybe what's actually requiring your attention now is a knot in your stomach. Or perhaps you're in a place you want to leave. Sometimes nothing “answers” and you just know what would be nice to do for yourself.
>
> And it might not be the same order all the time. You could have a feeling before thoughts, or thoughts might come first and provoke a feeling. They say hindsight is 20/20, but you actually might not even know which happened first. That's ok, too.

The suggestion to speak or write is useful because the person may feel more comfortable. The place they want to leave can be a real situation, not another category of inner signal. The final uncertainty is allowed to be ordinary. Notice that direct advice and reassurance survive in the positive version.

## 3. One difficulty carried through two situations

Source: the exact R8D and R8E candidate records linked in `CAMPAIGN-AND-RECOVERY.md`. These are model revisions within the successful multi-turn campaign, not natural-owner prose.

Before:

> Say you grew up learning that what you wanted didn't matter. Now you're finally figuring out what you think, maybe even learning to say no, and somebody tells you there is no self. Maybe years later that feels freeing. Right then it can sound like the old message all over again. Sometimes learning what you want and being able to say no has to come first.
>
> If you tell a therapist, “No, that's not right for me,” and they say the disagreement itself is resistance, then how could you ever tell them they're wrong? Maybe they've misunderstood you, or maybe what they're doing just isn't right for you. They have to be able to hear that without turning your no into more proof that they were right all along.

Successful regional revision, retained in the owner-reported 100% Human/high complete endpoint:

> Say you grew up learning that what you wanted didn't matter. You're only just starting to figure out what you actually think, maybe even learning to say no, and then somebody tells you there is no self—maybe freeing later, but right then it can sound like: what you want still doesn't matter. You say to a therapist, “No, that's not right for me,” and they call the disagreement resistance. What if they're wrong about you, or just taking you somewhere you don't want to go—how are you supposed to say so?

The ability to object is the live difficulty in both encounters. The second encounter carries it forward rather than restarting another miniature lesson. The ending question follows from the actual bind. This does not make questions a required ending.

### Catch the Hook Before the Story Takes Over

You might notice the hook before you have any clue what part of you is doing the hooking. Or you might notice it embarrassingly late. The jaw and stomach are already tight, and/or half the text is written, and then suddenly it dawns you: you're supposed to be observing yourself. 😀 Pema Chödrön calls this tightening and urge before the story has finished forming, "shenpa." Maybe forget the parts detective work for a minute. You can figure that out later.  

When you catch it, look around and just take a moment to enjoy some free honesty: "Oh! I'm hooked again! That's awesome I could notice it!" If nobody is in danger, take a breath or three before you finish whatever you were doing. Sometimes human beings need to be being, not always doing. 🙃 Maybe then you'll find there's enough adult around to be nice to the kid for a second. Or enough Protector to stop something you'd regret later.

Here's where I want to clearly depart from the new age wisdom that all your problems are self-created: Somebody may really have crossed a boundary and still have hit something old in you. You felt your stomach tighten up, and that somehow meant “They're evil.” But you can defer that judgement for when you're in your happy place.

## What the examples permit—not a recipe

The reader does not need a comprehensive guide at every heading. Give the current thought enough room to become understandable and useful. The examples show different valid possibilities: directly stating the remaining fact, following attention, carrying a difficulty into another encounter, and enjoying a discovery with the reader. Fit the choice to the actual target instead of making every paragraph perform all four.

The archive also contains Human results after deliberately adding explanatory aftercare, equalizing sentences, or adding a taxonomy alone. Those controls prevent turning stylistic preferences into universal Human/AI rules. Equally, a Human detector label does not make unnecessary prose worth keeping.
