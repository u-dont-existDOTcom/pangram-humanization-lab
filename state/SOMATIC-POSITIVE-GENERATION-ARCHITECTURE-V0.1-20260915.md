# Somatic positive generation architecture v0.1

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: PROVISIONAL GENERATION HYPOTHESIS / NOT ARTICLE AUTHORITY / NOT YET VALIDATED AS A PRODUCTION METHOD

## Problem this architecture is meant to solve

A preservation rule cannot humanize model-origin prose. The required method must take protected semantic/function content and generate a fresh Human-shaped realization without copying an already-Human target.

The latest Somatic hard-positive experiments show:

- visible model-shaped thought topology can remain Pangram Human;
- removing obvious colloquial/personal texture can still remain Human;
- context detachment, nominalization, and branch regularization can remain Human;
- a full polished-register normalization package flipped two Human paragraphs to AI-Assisted;
- decomposition was paragraph-dependent: P66 flipped from lexical/register normalization alone while editorial restructuring alone stayed Human; P17 stayed Human under either isolated subfactor even though the combined polished package flipped.

Therefore the generator should not optimize one scalar such as informality, roughness, irregular syntax, or low abstraction. The useful target is **locally appropriate realization diversity produced by the thought itself**.

## Core model

For each next sentence or clause, choose realization from local variables:

`realization_i = g(live_thought_i, speech_act_i, source_plane_i, stance_i, literal_context_i)`

Do not choose realization primarily from:

`global paragraph style -> professional consistency -> polished explanatory register`

The distinction is positive: it tells the writer what to generate from, not merely what to avoid.

## Generation procedure

### 1. Recover semantic custody without using it as a prose outline

Before writing, freeze the claims/functions that must survive, their certainty, attribution, actor/action/object, causal relations, and any protected wording. This is preservation input, not sentence order.

Do not compose by walking through the inventory item by item.

### 2. Identify the one locally live thought

Start from the literal preceding thought and ask what is actually being said or discovered next. A paragraph may contain several source planes, but only one thought should control the immediate next move.

### 3. Classify the local speech act

Examples include:

- **name / define** — introduce a technical term or distinction;
- **attribute / report** — say what a source, practitioner, study, or person claims;
- **explain** — state a causal or conceptual relation;
- **judge** — make the author's actual evaluation or disagreement;
- **instruct / advise** — tell the reader what to do or notice;
- **describe / observe** — state a concrete or lived observation;
- **qualify** — state a real uncertainty, boundary, or exception;
- **transition case/time** — move to a genuinely different situation, time, or use case.

This classification does not dictate a template. It selects what kind of language is natural for the move.

### 4. Let the object and speech act choose the register

Positive realization defaults:

- technical names may stay technical;
- technical ideas may be explained in ordinary language once named;
- source attribution should sound like reporting rather than generic academic synthesis;
- author judgments should use direct evaluative language when the source meaning contains a judgment;
- practical consequences and instructions should prefer ordinary action language;
- lived observations should retain their concrete terms rather than being translated upward into abstractions;
- evidence limitations should name the actual limitation rather than defaulting to generalized cautious prose;
- a sentence can legitimately mix a technical noun with ordinary surrounding language if that is the most natural expression of the thought.

Register shifts are therefore **content-conditioned**, not randomized or added as texture.

### 5. Generate locally, not by global harmonization

Produce the sentence that performs the current speech act and fits the literal preceding sentence. Then move to the next live thought.

Do not pre-plan a paragraph-wide cadence of `claim -> mechanism -> caveat -> takeaway`, and do not run a final pass whose mandate is to make diction, abstraction level, connective tissue, or sentence architecture consistent across the paragraph.

### 6. Stop when the thought stops

Do not manufacture synthesis merely because several claims have appeared. A close is earned only when the live question has actually changed or exhausted itself.

### 7. Repair defects locally

A critic may identify a specific coherence, fidelity, ambiguity, grammar, or placement problem. The repair should be local to that problem.

Do not hand the whole paragraph to a generic `make this smoother / clearer / more professional` rewrite pass. That operation is exactly the kind of global normalization the current detector evidence implicates.

## What this architecture is not

It does not instruct the model to:

- add mistakes;
- fake awkwardness;
- insert slang;
- invent autobiography or sensory detail;
- preserve genuine bad writing;
- alternate registers mechanically;
- use simpler words indiscriminately;
- imitate known Joel sentences;
- manipulate Pangram through phrase tricks.

The intended variation must be a byproduct of meaning, speech act, source plane, and stance.

## Production use on model-origin prose

For model-origin Somatic prose:

1. extract the protected meaning/functions and provenance;
2. discard the inherited model sentence realization as non-authoritative surface;
3. rebuild one live thought at a time using the procedure above;
4. preserve any already-owner-authored wording only where current article authority allows it;
5. run fidelity/architecture checks after assembly;
6. use Pangram only after the candidate is independently acceptable writing.

## Generative validation design

A real test must not merely mutate an owner-Human paragraph.

Choose a model-origin Somatic paragraph with no owner-Human target supplied to the generating context. Freeze one semantic/function packet and produce two fresh candidates:

- **Control:** conventional coherent rewrite with ordinary global stylistic consistency;
- **FCRA candidate:** function-conditioned realization architecture above.

Both candidates must preserve the same semantic units and pass a cold quality/fidelity check before detector testing.

Decision rule:

- if FCRA improves detector outcome without quality/fidelity loss, replicate on a second model-origin paragraph;
- if it does not, do not add more prohibitions; revise the generation architecture;
- do not promote FCRA into the Joel Articles production protocol until it succeeds generatively on more than one independent model-origin boundary.

## Current strongest open question

P17 shows that isolated lexical normalization or isolated restructuring can remain Human while their combined polished package becomes AI-Assisted. Therefore the Human signal may be relational/distributed: local realization choices interact across the paragraph.

A successful generator may need to preserve **independent local reasons for wording choices** rather than optimize any one feature distribution globally.
