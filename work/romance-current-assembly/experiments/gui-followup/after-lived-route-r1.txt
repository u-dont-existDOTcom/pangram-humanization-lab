Part of truth-telling after a breakup is admitting that the breakup itself may let you see things you genuinely couldn't see while bonded. But if all your new vision is aimed at your ex, that's suspicious. What did you honestly contribute? Which parts of her perspective were true? Sometimes what looked like one clean motive was actually her fighting with herself, and turning her into one simple thing afterward doesn't help you understand it.

Maybe she really was fake all along. Sometimes that's the most parsimonious explanation. It's also a wonderfully convenient story because it explains the whole relationship at once. This is when I want someone who isn't heavily invested in my innocence—a therapist, pastor, or even a stranger who can look at the evidence without needing me to be right.

Spiritual practice helps me do that without pretending I don't hurt. I can pray for my ex's wellbeing, remember what was real and good, and refuse to use my pain as permission to hate her. That doesn't mean accepting the New Age belief that everyone is simply a mirror of me. No, that isn't true.
