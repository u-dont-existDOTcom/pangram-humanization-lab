## The conversation about flaws

I wouldn’t ask someone out of the blue, “What did you do wrong in your past relationships?” That would be a pretty weird date.

But I would ask about their past relationships, especially the recent ones. If every story ends with everything being the other person’s fault, I’d ask, “Okay, so what lessons did you find for yourself?”

“I have baggage” tells me almost nothing. What are you having trouble with now? What might make it hard for you to function well in a relationship?

I’d want both of us to be honest about how effective we actually are at handling our own issues. What addictions or impulsive behaviors can’t we reliably control? How dependable are we? What are we probably going to ask forgiveness for more than once? That tells me more than either of us announcing that we’re healed.

The most recent conflicts are the best place to look. Were you able to regulate at all during the conflict? What did you do to the other person? How did you repair afterward? What did you learn? Has your judgment gotten better, or do you keep falling for the same stuff?

I’d also want to know what their friends or exes would say, because people aren’t always the best judges of their own progress.

And if somebody tells me, “I shut down,” okay, what does that mean? Did you stop talking to your partner for days? Did they feel abandoned?

My own list isn’t especially flattering. I’m impulsive. I idealize too much, although I don’t demonize. I’m very open and not very private. I care more about honesty than the standard romantic ideal. I still love my exes. After being deeply in love, I have overly high expectations, so I’m hard to please with a basic relationship.

I’m also extremely unique and have deeply held convictions many people won’t agree with. I’m anti-vax, anti-money, anti-civilization, etc. And I’m not yet living the way I want to live.

A woman should know that before she starts fitting me into the life or fantasy she already planned.

Key was an inner-child therapist and Reiki healer. Both Key and Bee were extremely curious and open-minded, which I loved about them. They were open to spiritual experiences, very connected to childlike wonder, and were always seeing what they needed to work on. They both meditated a lot.

I saw all that and thought anyone with that much spiritual depth and curiosity must already be far along on the path.

That was my mistake. Their meditations may have helped them a lot. I still had no idea how either woman would act when a relationship got ugly. If somebody starts with enough trauma, huge progress can still leave a lot unresolved.

My own blind spot is that I have trouble understanding where people are at mentally and emotionally. A partner may agree with everything I say and then tell me later, “Didn’t you understand I was FAWNING?”

No, I really didn’t. If you’re fawning, tell me when you can, because I can’t know what’s happening in your head just because it feels obvious to you.
