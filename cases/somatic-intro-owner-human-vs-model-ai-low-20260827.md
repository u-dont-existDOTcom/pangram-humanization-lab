Date: 2026-08-27

## Owner-supplied Human exemplars

Joel reported both of the following as Pangram Human. He prefers the first.

### Human exemplar 1

Physical trauma reactions can continue after the danger is over, even when you know you're safe.

With complex or developmental trauma, going straight for the deepest memory can be overwhelming. Instead of diving off the deep end heroically, take it easy, one step at a time. As Buddhist nun Pema Chodron says, "feather touch" is all it takes to gently open up.  Peter Levine, the founder of Somatic Experiencing, calls this gentle work around the edges, "pendulation."

In inner-child therapy, you can know exactly what the younger part needs and still get pulled so far into the child-state that there isn't enough adult perspective left to give it.

### Human exemplar 2

Physical trauma reactions can continue long after the danger is over.  With complex or developmental trauma, going straight for the deepest memory can be overwhelming.   In inner-child therapy, you can know just what the younger you needs and still get pulled so far into the child-state that you end up retraumatized.  That's where somatic therapy comes to the rescue.

## Model attempts generated after inspecting those Human exemplars

### Attempt A

Physical trauma reactions don't necessarily disappear just because the danger did.

Going after the worst memory first can make complex trauma worse. Pema Chodron has this lovely phrase, “feather touch.” You don't need to smash the door down. Peter Levine gets at something similar with pendulation in Somatic Experiencing.

Inner-child work gets tricky for the same reason. You can know what the younger part needs and get so lost in being that child that nobody is left to take care of them.

### Attempt B

A person can be perfectly safe now and still have their body freaking out about something that happened years ago.

So I don't see the point of treating the deepest trauma memory like Mount Everest. With complex trauma especially, gentler is often smarter. Pema Chodron calls it a “feather touch.” Somatic Experiencing has its own version of this in pendulation.

This matters a lot for inner-child work, because getting completely sucked into the child-state is not reparenting. You need enough adult left to actually do the parenting.

### Attempt C

Sometimes the problem with trauma therapy is that people try way too hard.

The body can keep reacting after the danger is gone, and then somebody decides the answer is to go straight into the worst memory they have. Why? Pema Chodron's “feather touch” makes a lot more sense to me. Somatic Experiencing does something similar with pendulation: don't force the whole thing open at once.

And if you're doing inner-child work, this becomes pretty obvious. Get swallowed by the child-state and now you've got two children and no parent.

## Interpretation

This falsifies a simple feature-recipe theory. The Human exemplars contain several visible traits — selective rather than exhaustive explanation, colloquial idiom, stronger stance, named authorities, uneven development, less hedging, less explanatory aftercare — but deliberately synthesizing those traits did not reproduce Human classification.

The most useful production conclusion is not to keep inventing fresh model variants. Exact owner Human prose should be preferred and preserved. When a Human owner passage is missing a necessary article function, use minimum-dose editing around or within it rather than regenerating the complete natural boundary. Treat the difference between owner Human realization and model imitation as distributed/idiosyncratic unless controlled evidence localizes a narrower cause.

Do not promote this to a universal Pangram mechanism. The exact outcomes are short-boundary, owner-reported detector evidence.
